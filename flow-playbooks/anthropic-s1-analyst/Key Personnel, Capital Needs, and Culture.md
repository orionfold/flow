#### Key Personnel, Capital Needs, and Culture

We are highly dependent on the continued service of our co-founders, senior management team, and key technical employees, and the loss of any of these individuals may adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

We are highly dependent on the continued service of our Chief Executive Officer, President, co-founders, senior management team, and key technical team. In particular, our Chief Executive Officer and President are critical to setting our strategic direction, advancing our research agenda, shaping our safety and governance frameworks, and generally advancing our mission, and we believe that their leadership would be difficult to replace. If either were to become unavailable for any reason, or if either of their reputations were significantly harmed, regardless of merit, there could be an adverse impact on our operations and competitive position, employee morale, employee retention, pace of innovation, customer and partner confidence in our company, our brand and reputation, and our long-term strategic objectives. Further, the loss of key personnel, including members of our founding team and senior management team, and key engineering, research, product development, marketing, and sales personnel, could also disrupt our operations and have an adverse effect on our business. Many of our key personnel, particularly within our technical and research teams, are strongly motivated by our mission and the opportunity to work on addressing specific problems within the AI landscape. To the extent that their interests evolve, they seek to pursue different technical challenges or areas of focus, or they are drawn to other organizations or initiatives that they perceive as better aligned with their personal beliefs or professional objectives, we may face increased difficulty retaining such personnel. This risk may be heightened as we continue to scale and mature as an organization, which may change the nature of the work environment, priorities, or perceived mission alignment for certain personnel, including personnel who may conclude that the objectives that drew them to us have been substantially achieved. In addition, under certain of our agreements with suppliers, the loss of certain key personnel could accelerate our obligations or otherwise trigger adverse consequences under such agreements.

If we are unable to retain key personnel, or if we fail to conduct effective succession planning and find suitable replacements for any losses of such key personnel in a timely manner and on competitive terms, we could experience declines in sales, delays in research and development, and harm to our business and operations. If we are unable to manage the foregoing risks, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.

We may require additional capital to meet our substantial ongoing obligations and support our business growth, and our ability to generate sufficient revenue and secure additional capital on acceptable terms is critical to our continued operations and competitive position.

Our business model requires substantial and increasing capital to support our ability to remain competitive and to stay at the frontier of AI. We intend to continue to make significant financial investments to support our business growth and may require additional funds to acquire sufficient compute and respond to business challenges, including expenditures to create, maintain, upgrade, and enhance our models, platform, and applications, including costs related to investments in infrastructure, human capital, office expansion, and data assets. In addition, we may use a portion of our cash to satisfy tax withholding and remittance obligations related to outstanding RSUs and equity awards that we grant in the future.

In addition, to date we have financed our compute primarily under arrangements with CSPs, with costs recognized as compute is made available to us. We may in the future finance a greater portion of our compute on an up-front basis, including through capital leases, prepayments, debt-financed purchases of equipment, or similar arrangements. If we were to do so, we may incur significant cash outflows in advance of compute availability or related revenue, which could increase demands on our cash flows and liquidity and our need to raise additional capital. Such arrangements could also increase our capital intensity and alter our cash-flow profile.

Accordingly, it is critical that we maintain strong revenue growth, grow revenue efficiently relative to our committed compute, infrastructure, and personnel costs, and manage our cash flows effectively. To the extent that such efforts are insufficient to meet our capital needs, we may need to obtain additional capital through equity or debt financings. If we raise additional funds through future issuances of equity, equity-linked securities, or convertible debt securities, our existing stockholders could suffer dilution, and any new securities we issue could have rights, preferences, and privileges superior to those of holders of our common stock. Further, the conversion of our outstanding convertible securities will cause additional dilution to our existing

stockholders. Any debt financing that we may secure in the future may involve restrictive covenants relating to our capital raising activities and other financial and operational matters, potentially making it more difficult for us to obtain additional capital and to pursue business opportunities.

In addition, our ability to draw amounts under certain of our note purchase agreements (“NPAs”) is not guaranteed and is subject to the satisfaction of specified conditions. In particular, our ability to access additional amounts under certain of these facilities depends on our achievement of certain revenue performance targets and/or the ability of third parties to deliver additional compute to us. While we have historically surpassed all such revenue performance targets, there can be no assurance that revenue will continue to exceed these thresholds at all times in the future. If these conditions are not satisfied, we may be unable to draw additional amounts under the applicable NPA, which could adversely affect our cash flows and liquidity. For more information about the terms of our convertible promissory notes, see the section titled “Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations—Liquidity and Capital Resources—Convertible Notes.”

We may not be able to obtain additional financing on terms favorable to us, if at all. We evaluate financing opportunities from time to time, and our ability to obtain financing will depend, among other things, on our development efforts, business plans, and operating performance and the condition of the capital markets at the time we seek financing. The equity and debt markets may experience extreme volatility and disruption from time to time, which could result in higher credit spreads in such markets and higher funding costs for us. If we are unable to generate sufficient revenue growth or obtain adequate financing or financing on terms satisfactory to us when we require it, our ability to continue to support our business growth, scale our infrastructure, develop new models, platforms, and applications, enhance our existing models, platform, and applications, and respond to business challenges may be impaired, and our business, financial condition, and results of operations may be adversely affected.

Our company culture has contributed to our success and, if we cannot maintain this culture as we grow, our business could be adversely affected.

We believe that our company culture is a critical component to our success. We are a mission-driven company and our culture reflects a strong emphasis on the Company Purpose. Our ability to continue to cultivate and maintain this culture is essential to our growth and continued success. We face a number of challenges associated with our commitment to our company culture, including:

•the need to identify, attract, reward, and retain people in leadership positions in our organization who

share and further our culture, values, and mission;

•the increasing size and geographic diversity of our workforce, and our ability to promote a uniform and

consistent culture across all of our offices and employees;

•internal and external perceptions about our mission, values, commitment to safety, and uses of our

models, platform, and applications;

•engagement with government policymakers on issues that resonate differently across our workforce;

•competitive pressures to move in directions that may divert us from our mission and values;

•the rigorous, values-driven standards we apply in hiring and decision-making, which may slow the

pace at which we can scale our workforce or expand our operations relative to competitors;

•the continued challenges of a rapidly evolving industry;

•the increased scrutiny and expectations associated with operating as a public company; and

•the need to balance our long-term, mission-driven objectives with shorter-term financial performance

expectations, which may create internal tension and challenge our ability to consistently reinforce our cultural values and mission across teams and leadership.

Our mission-driven culture is one of our core characteristics that helps us to attract and retain key personnel and differentiate us in the market. If we are unable to maintain our culture, as well as balance our commitment to maintaining our culture with the demands of rapid growth, competition, and operating as a public company, our employee morale, engagement, and retention could suffer, requiring us to incur additional costs or adopt alternative recruiting strategies, which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.
