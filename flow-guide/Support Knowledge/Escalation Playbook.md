# Escalation Playbook

**Fictional service policy · adapt owners, hours and response targets before use**

## Keep a named owner as the problem moves

The support owner remains the customer's contact while the specialist investigates. Escalation adds expertise and an explicit response target; it does not silently transfer accountability.

| Trigger | Route | Example first-response target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Possible data loss or exposure | On-call incident lead | 15 minutes during the defined coverage window |
| Three matching reports in seven days | Engineering triage | Same business day |
| Customer repeats an unresolved request | Support lead | Two business hours |
| Billing dispute | Finance owner | Next business day |
| Contract interpretation | Account owner and legal reviewer | Next business day |

Targets are illustrative internal expectations, not customer SLAs. Add an after-hours route and incident policy before operational use.

## The handoff packet

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Issue and impact | Observable behavior, affected work and urgency |
| Evidence | Dated source IDs, reproduction and actions already attempted |
| Ownership | Support owner and accepting specialist |
| Next contact | A promised time and the person responsible |
| Resolution | Verified outcome and any reusable knowledge gap |

Feed repeated questions into [[Support Knowledge]] after review. Remove identifying customer details from reusable articles and distinguish a proposed answer from an approved one.
