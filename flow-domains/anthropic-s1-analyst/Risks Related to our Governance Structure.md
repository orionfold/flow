## Risks Related to our Governance Structure

Our capital stock structure features certain provisions that are novel or otherwise not common among other corporations with multiple-class structures, and may effectively eliminate the ability of holders of our Class A common stock to influence important decisions, including a change in control.

We have a novel capital and corporate governance structure that has no direct public company precedent. We are incorporated as a PBC under Delaware law. As a PBC, we are required to balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose identified in our amended and restated certificate of incorporation. In addition, the LTBT possesses certain rights that are designed to help ensure that we are managed in a manner that responsibly balances our goals as a PBC. Through a special class of common stock, Class T common stock, the LTBT generally has the authority to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. See the section titled “Our Governance Structure and Its Goals—Long-Term Benefit Trust— Election and Removal of Directors” for more information. The LTBT is intended to operate as an independent body of five financially disinterested Trustees, and our Class T common stock has no economic rights. As a result, the LTBT may have interests that differ from yours, and it may exercise its power in a way with which

Immediately prior to the completion of this offering, we will also issue a single share of Class F common stock, a special class of our common stock, to the Founder LLC. Our Class F common stock will be entitled to an aggregate number of votes that equals 50.1% of the total voting power of our outstanding shares of capital stock as of the applicable record date with respect to matters submitted to stockholders generally, including the election of our directors other than the Class T directors. Upon the completion of this offering, we will also authorize a special class of our common stock, Class E common stock, which we will issue to our employees, excluding our co-founders. Our Class E common stock will be entitled to vote only on certain limited matters and will not have any economic rights. Under this governance structure, we expect that the LTBT and the Founder LLC will collectively have the ability to elect all members of our board of directors, and the Founder LLC will have the ability to determine the outcome of all matters submitted to a vote of our stockholders generally for the foreseeable future. Accordingly, holders of our Class A common stock generally will not be able to affect the outcome of any matter submitted to a vote of our stockholders or the election of our directors, regardless of how they vote, unless a majority of the Continuing Founders (as defined below) cannot agree on how our Class F common stock will be voted (in which case our Class F common stock will generally be voted proportionately with the votes of our Class A common stock cast on the matter).

Our governance structure is likely to have the effect of limiting the likelihood of an unsolicited merger proposal, unsolicited tender offer, or proxy contest for the removal of directors. As a result, our governance structure may make it more difficult to replace our directors and management and may have the effect of depriving our stockholders of an opportunity to sell their shares at a premium over prevailing market prices.

We cannot predict whether our structure will result in lower demand for our Class A common stock, increased volatility, adverse publicity, or other adverse consequences. In particular, the absence of judicial precedent interpreting this structure increases the risk and unpredictability of governance-related litigation, and courts may interpret the obligations inherent in our structure in ways that we might not anticipate.

Our capital structure will concentrate voting power with the LTBT and the Founder LLC for the foreseeable future and will result in the LTBT and the Founder LLC having voting power that is not derived from or proportionate to economic interests in our company.

Our Class T common stock will generally entitle the LTBT to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. Our Class T common stock will remain outstanding indefinitely, subject to the right of our board of directors and the Trustees or specified majorities of our Class A common stock, Class E common stock, and Class F common stock to amend or terminate the terms of the LTBT or the rights of our Class T common stock or to redeem our Class T common stock (such time as no share of Class T common stock is outstanding, the “Class T Sunset Time”). The Class T common stock may also be transferred without restriction upon the receipt of the same approvals required to amend, terminate, or redeem it. We intentionally structured the Class T common stock in this manner to preserve long-term flexibility if circumstances change. If the Class T common stock were transferred to a person or entity that is not affiliated with the LTBT or is not otherwise required to consider the Company Purpose, for so long as we remain a PBC, all of our directors, regardless of how they are elected, would continue to be obligated to act in accordance with Delaware law and our certificate of incorporation, bylaws, and other governing documents as then in effect, including those provisions relating to our public benefit purpose. The Trust Agreement (as defined below) limits Trustee compensation to a reasonable cash amount fixed annually by the Trustees. Trustee compensation will not vary with or be conditioned upon the profits or any action or inaction of our company. The policies of the LTBT prohibit a Trustee from directly purchasing or holding any of our securities.

Our Class F common stock will entitle the Founder LLC to an aggregate number of votes that equals 50.1% of the total voting power of our outstanding shares of capital stock as of the applicable record date with respect to matters submitted to stockholders generally, including the election of our directors other than the Class T directors. This divergence of voting power from economic interests in our company is likely to persist for the foreseeable future and will not be subject to dilution based on additional issuances of our capital stock or reduction for sales or dispositions of our capital stock by our individual co-founders. Our governance structure and the adoption of our amended and restated certificate of incorporation may negatively affect the decision by certain institutional investors to purchase or hold shares of our Class A common stock.

Certain aspects of our governance structure, such as our multi-class capital structure, may limit the ability of certain institutional investors to invest in us as a result of their investment policies or may make such an investment less attractive to the portfolio managers of certain institutional investors.

In addition, our multi-class capital structure may make us ineligible for inclusion in indices, and as a result, mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, and other investment vehicles that attempt to passively track these indices would not invest in our stock. These policies may depress our valuation compared to those of other similar companies that are able to be included in such indices.

We are considered a “controlled company” within the meaning of the Nasdaq Global Select Market corporate governance requirements, and, as a result, we qualify for, and currently rely on, exemptions from certain corporate governance requirements.

We are considered a “controlled company” within the meaning of the Nasdaq Global Select Market corporate governance requirements because the LTBT, as the sole holder of our Class T common stock, generally has the power to elect a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. We operate as a Delaware public benefit corporation. As a public benefit corporation, we cannot provide any assurance that we will achieve our Company Purpose.

As a PBC, we are intended to produce a public benefit or benefits and to operate in a responsible and sustainable manner, balancing our stockholders’ pecuniary interests, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose identified in our amended and restated certificate of incorporation. We cannot assure you that we will achieve our Company Purpose or that the expected positive impact from being a PBC will be realized, which could adversely affect our reputation, business, financial condition, and results of operations. As a PBC, we are required, under Section 366 of the Delaware General Corporation Law (“DGCL”) and pursuant to our amended and restated bylaws, to disclose to stockholders a report at least biennially on our overall performance in promoting the public benefit identified in our amended and restated certificate of incorporation and the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct and on our assessment of our success in achieving our Company Purpose and in promoting those interests. If we are not timely or are unable to provide this report, or if the report is not viewed favorably by parties doing business with us, regulators, others reviewing our credentials, or other stakeholders, including holders of our Class A common stock, our reputation as a PBC may be adversely affected.

Our focus on the long-term best interests of our company as a PBC, and the duty of our directors to balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those stakeholders materially affected by our conduct, and our Company Purpose, may conflict with short-, medium-, or long-term financial interests and business performance, which may negatively impact the value of our Class A common stock.

We have made, and may in the future make, decisions that we believe balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose set forth in our amended and restated certificate of incorporation, even if such decisions may negatively impact the short-, medium-, or long-term performance of our business, financial condition, and results of operations or the short-, medium-, or long-term performance of our Class A common stock. This may impact the value of our Class A common stock and make owning our Class A common stock less appealing to investors who are focused on returns. Our decisions and actions in pursuit of long-term success may not result in the long-term benefits we expect, which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations, as well as the market price of our Class A common stock. Such decisions and actions may include changes to our models, platform, and applications; limitations on certain uses of our models, platform, and applications; moderating the pace of the development of our models for safety-related reasons; adjustments to the timing of new offerings; changes in how we deliver customer support; investments in our relationships with partners and employees; investments in and introductions of new models, platforms, and applications; investments in social impact initiatives consistent with our public benefit objectives; and changes in our approach to working with local or national jurisdictions on laws and regulations governing our business.

Our policies, including those on usage, currently prohibit, or may be revised in the future to prohibit, certain usage or other actions, which may have adverse commercial consequences to us. For example, our usage policy prohibits the use of our models, platform, and applications to develop or operate fully autonomous weapons or to conduct mass surveillance, including mass domestic surveillance, and we have declined to permit such uses based on our Company Purpose, notwithstanding the revenue those or similar uses could generate. Maintaining these limitations has had, and may continue to have, adverse commercial and financial consequences. We have also restricted or delayed the availability of certain capabilities for safety reasons. For example, we initially released our Mythos model only as a gated research preview through Project Glasswing rather than making it broadly available to the public. Similarly, we have chosen not to develop certain commercially attractive offerings, such as image and video generation models, in order to direct our compute toward our research and safety priorities. Our public advocacy, and the decisions we make to moderate the pace of AI development, delay or restrict the release of models or features, or forgo commercial opportunities for safety-related reasons, may not maximize stockholder value, could have a negative impact on the value of our Class A common stock, and could result in litigation. Each of these decisions reflects our directors’ balancing of the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose.

While we believe these decisions are consistent with the Company Purpose, as a result of these decisions, we may forgo revenue, customers, or growth opportunities that a corporation managed solely to maximize stockholder value might pursue.

Further, while directors of traditional corporations typically have a fiduciary duty to focus on maximizing stockholder value, directors of a PBC have a duty to consider not only the stockholders’ financial interests but also the company’s specific public benefit purpose and the best interests of those stakeholders materially affected by the company’s actions. Thus, our directors are not merely permitted, but obligated, to consider our Company Purpose and the interests of other stakeholders. Under Delaware law, directors are shielded from liability for breach of these obligations if they make informed and disinterested decisions that serve a rational purpose. In the event of a conflict among the interests of our stockholders, our Company Purpose or the interests of our other stakeholders, our directors must only make informed and disinterested decisions that serve a rational purpose; thus, we cannot guarantee that such a conflict would be resolved in favor of our stockholders’ monetary interests, which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations and cause the market price of our Class A common stock to decline. In addition, there is very limited judicial precedent interpreting the balancing requirement applicable to PBCs under Delaware law, particularly for companies with significant scale or visibility, or with a scope of PBC purpose similar to ours. As a result, the scope of directors’ obligations, the standards by which their decisions may be reviewed, and the nature and availability of stockholder remedies in connection with alleged failures to responsibly balance these interests remain uncertain. Future judicial interpretations of these provisions could be more expansive than we currently anticipate and could result in increased litigation risk, greater scrutiny of board decision-making, or outcomes that differ from our expectations.

Additionally, as a PBC, we are less vulnerable to an unsolicited takeover effort than a traditional company would be and, therefore, your ability to realize your investment through an acquisition may be limited. We may also not be an attractive target for activists or hedge fund investors because new directors must consider and give appropriate weight to the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and our Company Purpose, and stockholders may be able to enforce this obligation through litigation. Further, by requiring that our board of directors consider additional stakeholders and our Company Purpose, and not just maximizing stockholder value, Delaware law could make it easier for our board of directors to reject a hostile bid, even where the acceptance of such a bid would provide the greatest short- term financial yield to investors.

In addition, we intend to direct a meaningful portion of our resources toward our public benefit initiatives, which may include contributions to nonprofit organizations, research institutions, underserved communities, and other third parties that may not otherwise have access to frontier AI capabilities. These initiatives, which include the Initial Public Benefit Issuance and the future Public Benefit Issuances, have been, and may continue to be, substantial in scale, and we expect to significantly grow these efforts over time. The benefits of such expenditures may be difficult or impossible to measure in financial terms and may not result in any direct or measurable benefit to our business or our stockholders. The amount of resources directed toward these initiatives may, in any given period, reduce our revenue, increase our expenses, and could otherwise affect our results of operations.

As a Delaware public benefit corporation, we may be subject to increased litigation concerning our board of directors’ obligation to balance our stockholders’ pecuniary interests, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct and our Company Purpose, the occurrence of which may have an adverse impact on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Section 367 of the DGCL provides that stockholders of a Delaware PBC with shares listed on a national securities exchange may only file an action to enforce the statutory balancing requirement imposed on the board of directors by the DGCL if they, individually or collectively, own the lesser of at least two percent of the corporation’s outstanding shares or shares of the corporation with a market value of at least $2,000,000 as of the date the action is instituted and, in the case of a derivative action, such stockholders satisfy any other conditions applicable to filing a derivative action. This potential liability for enforcement of the statutory balancing requirement does not exist for traditional corporations. Therefore, we may be subject to the possibility of increased litigation, which would require the attention of our management, and, as a result, may adversely affect our management’s ability to effectively execute our strategy. There is limited judicial precedent

Our amended and restated bylaws will designate a state or federal court located within the State of Delaware as the exclusive forum for substantially all disputes between us and our stockholders, and also provide that the federal district courts will be the exclusive forum for resolving any complaint asserting a cause of action arising under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, each of which may limit our stockholders’ ability to choose the judicial forum for disputes with us or our directors, officers, stockholders, or employees.

Our amended and restated bylaws, which will become effective immediately prior to the completion of this offering, will provide that, unless we consent in writing to the selection of an alternative forum, the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, if the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware does not have jurisdiction, another State court in Delaware or the federal district court for the District of Delaware) will, to the fullest extent permitted by law, be the sole and exclusive forum for (i) any derivative action or proceeding brought on our behalf, (ii) any action asserting a claim of breach of a fiduciary duty owed by any of our current or former directors, stockholders, or officers or other employees to us or our stockholders, (iii) any action arising pursuant to any provision of the DGCL, or as to which the DGCL confers jurisdiction upon the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware, (iv) any action arising pursuant to or to interpret, apply, enforce or determine the validity of our amended and restated certificate of incorporation, or our amended and restated bylaws, or (v) any action asserting a claim governed by the internal affairs doctrine, except for, in all cases, any claim as to which such court determines that there is an indispensable party not subject to the jurisdiction of such court (and the indispensable party does not consent to the personal jurisdiction of such court within 10 days following such determination).

Section 22 of the Securities Act of 1933, as amended (the “Securities Act”), establishes concurrent jurisdiction for federal and state courts over Securities Act claims. Accordingly, both state and federal courts have jurisdiction to hear such claims. To prevent having to litigate claims in multiple jurisdictions and the threat of inconsistent or contrary rulings by different courts, among other considerations, our amended and restated bylaws will also provide that, unless we consent in writing to the selection of an alternative forum, the federal district courts of the United States will, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, be the sole and exclusive forum for resolving any complaint asserting a cause or causes of action arising under the Securities Act against any person in connection with any offering of our securities, including, without limitation, any auditor, underwriter, expert, control person, or other defendant. Unless we consent in writing to the selection of an alternative forum, the United States District Court for the District of Delaware will, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, be the sole and exclusive forum for the resolution of any derivative action or proceeding brought to enforce any duty or liability created by the Exchange Act or the rules or regulations thereunder. All other claims arising under the Exchange Act may be brought in any federal court of competent jurisdiction.

Any person or entity purchasing, holding, or otherwise acquiring any interest in any of our securities shall be deemed to have notice of and consented to the foregoing bylaw provisions. Although we believe these exclusive forum provisions benefit us by providing increased consistency in the application of Delaware law and federal securities laws in the types of lawsuits to which each applies, the exclusive forum provisions may limit a stockholder’s ability to bring a claim in a judicial forum of its choosing for disputes with us or our current or former directors, officers, stockholders, or other employees, which may discourage lawsuits with respect to such claims against us and our current and former directors, officers, stockholders, or other employees. Our stockholders will not be deemed to have waived our compliance with the federal securities laws and the rules and regulations thereunder as a result of our exclusive forum provisions.
