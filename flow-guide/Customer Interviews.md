# Eleven conversations

Between 8 and 26 September we spoke to eleven customers: six enterprise, five self-serve, chosen to over-represent recent expansion rather than to be representative. Every quote here is used with permission and lightly trimmed for length.

## What we asked

1. Walk me through the last document you made that mattered.
2. Who else touched it, and when?
3. What did you do when you did not trust what you were reading?

The third question was added after the second interview, because the first two both volunteered an answer to it.

## What came back

| Theme | Enterprise (6) | Self-serve (5) | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Expanded seats without being sold | 5 | 1 | The retention signal |
| Trust checking is manual today | 6 | 4 | Screenshots into chat |
| Wanted the file, not the app | 4 | 5 | Near-universal |
| Mentioned price unprompted | 0 | 0 | Notable given [[Market Landscape]] |
| Asked for an approval trail | 5 | 0 | Cleanly split by segment |

The last row is the most actionable thing in this document. Every enterprise account asked, unprompted, for a record of what changed and who agreed to it. No self-serve user did.

## In their words

> We pasted the draft into a chat tool, asked it to check the numbers, then copied the answer back into the doc. Then we did it again the next week because nobody could remember whether we had.
>
> — Operations lead, 340-seat account

> I do not want another place my work lives. I want my folder to get smarter.
>
> — Independent consultant, self-serve

> The reason we could not use [a suite] is that legal would not sign off on where the documents sat. That was the whole evaluation.
>
> — Head of finance, 120-seat account

## What we are not concluding

Eleven interviews cannot tell us why self-serve conversion halved, because everyone in the sample already converted. The people who would explain that number are the ones who left the pricing page, and we did not talk to any of them. [[Market Landscape]] treats the price hypothesis as unresolved for exactly this reason.

Nor can this sample speak to churn: we deliberately over-sampled expansion. Read it as evidence about people who stayed.
