#### Internal Governance and Decision-Making

To institutionalize our safety commitments and ensure we are managed in a manner that responsibly balances our tripartite goals as a PBC, we have established the LTBT, which through a special class of common stock, Class T common stock, generally has the authority to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. In addition to the LTBT and our structure as a PBC, we train our models in accordance with Claude’s constitution, which we make publicly available, and have adopted and continue to update safety policies, such as our Responsible Scaling Policy. We also solicit and seriously consider input from a wide range of stakeholders and constituents, including our stockholders, employees, customers, the LTBT, industry leaders and experts, and AI safety organizations. Such practices and processes have previously resulted in, and may in the future result in, slower decision-making and product releases. We may also face potential conflicts when safety and business interests diverge. If we do not successfully manage these risks related to our AI safety processes and practices, or if there is a perception that we have not successfully managed such risks, we may face reputational harm, internal conflicts resulting in a deterioration of our company culture, slower or inferior product releases, or litigation. For more information, see the section titled “—Risks Related to our Governance Structure.”

We have implemented an external reviewer program and engage researchers, adversarial testers, and other third parties to support aspects of our safety, security, and model-evaluation efforts. Certain of these arrangements are novel and may require us to provide such parties with access to internal information, systems, personnel, or models, which could increase the risk of disclosure of confidential, proprietary, or other sensitive information. In addition, findings, assessments, or public statements by such third parties may differ from our views and could adversely affect customer trust, regulatory perceptions, or our reputation.

Heightened Reputational Vulnerability. As we have publicly positioned ourselves as a safety-focused

organization, we face heightened vulnerability to reputational harm from any perceived inconsistency between our stated values and actual practices. We are held to higher standards than certain competitors that have not made similar public commitments, and any incidents impacting our perception as a safety-focused organization could result in:

•Employee Disruption. Employees who joined our company based on our safety commitments may

experience a decline in morale, disillusionment, a loss of trust in leadership, or reduced productivity if they perceive we are not living up to our stated values. Public disagreements regarding our safety approach, including criticism from current or former employees, or the departure of personnel associated with our safety efforts, could amplify such perceptions. Such negative perceptions could also impair our ability to recruit and hire new talent to the organization. Any erosion of our safety-focused reputation could also make it more difficult or costly to attract and retain highly skilled employees, including by increasing compensation or other hiring costs. These dynamics could lead to increased turnover, loss of institutional knowledge, and reduced effectiveness of our teams, which could in turn cause disruption to ongoing development and safety initiatives and impair our ability to remain at the frontier of AI model development.

•Customer Backlash. Customers and enterprise partners who choose to deploy our models, platform,

and applications based on our safety positioning may lose trust in us, terminate or decline to renew their contracts, demand refunds, publicly criticize our company, or switch to our competitors, which could result in harm to our reputation, revenue loss, and reduced market share. These risks may be heightened with respect to programs in which we make models available only to a limited group of vetted organizations, such as Project Glasswing. Organizations not selected for, or removed from, such programs may perceive our access decisions as arbitrary, exclusionary, or anticompetitive, which could result in reputational harm, loss of customer or partner relationships, regulatory complaints, or litigation, including claims under antitrust or unfair competition laws. Conversely, participants in such programs may publicly criticize us or terminate their participation if program terms, eligibility criteria, transition arrangements to successor models, or capability levels do not meet their expectations.

In addition, we have engaged with governments in Canada, the EU, the UK, the United States, and other jurisdictions in connection with Project Glasswing and related frontier-capability programs, and we have positioned such programs, in part, to help provide the foundation for broader industry coordination on the responsible release of frontier models with cybersecurity and other advanced capabilities. The commercial viability of these and similar future programs may depend on sustained governmental and industry coalition support. If such coalitions fail to materialize, are perceived as exclusionary or anticompetitive, are rolled back, or result in mandates we cannot readily satisfy (including with respect to access, localization, monitoring, or reporting), our ability to commercialize models with Mythos-class capabilities, and the reputational benefits we derive from safety-focused positioning, could be materially impaired. For more information, see the section titled “—If we are unable to maintain and enhance our brand and reputation, our business, financial condition, and results of operations may be adversely affected.”

We voluntarily publicly disclose information regarding AI safety challenges, research findings, and limitations of our models and systems, that exceeds standard industry practice and regulatory requirements. While our intent with these voluntary disclosures is to advance the safe development of AI and build public trust, these disclosures create several risks. The AI safety landscape is nascent and evolving rapidly, and our understanding of and perspective on appropriate safety practices, risk tradeoffs, and deployment contexts may change over time as technologies, use cases, and regulatory expectations develop. As a result, statements we make today may later prove incomplete, require modification, or be interpreted differently in light of subsequent developments. To the extent we adhere to prior public statements or frameworks, we may also limit our operational flexibility, product development, or deployment decisions, even where evolving circumstances would otherwise warrant a different approach. Our public statements about AI risks, safety challenges, areas of uncertainty, or potential societal impacts, including statements regarding job displacement or economic disruption, may be used against us in litigation, regulatory proceedings, or public controversies, particularly if our future actions or statements are viewed as inconsistent with our prior statements and commitments. Regulators have and could continue to use our disclosures as the basis for heightened scrutiny or more stringent requirements applicable to us compared to less transparent competitors. Additionally, our disclosures have provided, and may in the future provide, competitors with insights into our proprietary training and other sensitive processes, research priorities, and strategic concerns, without reciprocal transparency, which may be potentially competitively disadvantageous to us. If we are unable to effectively manage the foregoing risks, our brand, reputation, and ability to compete could be adversely affected.

Resource Allocation and Scaling. Safety-focused development is resource-intensive. We must allocate limited

resources, capital, and talent between safety-focused workloads and other initiatives, often without clear returns on our safety-focused investments. For example, our investments in interpretability research and techniques designed to ensure our models’ behavior remains consistent with intended goals, values, and safety objectives may not yield actual or perceived commercial returns. Additionally, as we develop our models, platform, and applications, safety testing may become more expensive and resource intensive. Limited-access programs such as Project Glasswing may also require us to design, operate, and continually maintain bespoke vetting, contracting, monitoring, and offboarding processes that differ materially from our standard commercial release processes, including coordination among participating organizations, transition planning to

If we are unable to maintain and enhance our brand and reputation, our business, financial condition, and results of operations may be adversely affected.

We believe that maintaining and enhancing our brand identity and reputation is critical to our relationships with, and to our ability to attract and retain, customers, partners, and employees. Our brand and reputation are influenced not only by our actions, but also by the actions of our partners, our customers, and other third parties with whom we do business, and by broader public perceptions of the AI industry, and we may be grouped with other industry participants and judged similarly, regardless of differences in our approach or conduct. The successful promotion of our brand depends on a number of factors, including our ability to continue to develop and deliver reliable, high-quality, and responsible models, platforms, and applications that meet the needs of our customers; our sales and marketing efforts, including our effectiveness at developing a strong brand name and positive presence within the AI industry through participation in industry events and key media coverage; the perceptions and experiences of both our enterprise and individual customers, including through testimonials, use cases, and word-of-mouth; our ability to maintain security and reliability of our models, platform, and applications; the actions of our management; and our ability to differentiate our models, platform, and applications from those of our competitors, including through product design choices, safeguards, business models, and policies, including our approach to monetization strategies that differ from those of certain competitors. In addition, our brand is subject to heightened scrutiny with respect to perceived societal, environmental, labor, health, and safety-related impacts of AI technologies, and a single perceived failure could result in outsized reputational harm. For example, as AI systems become more capable or prevalent, public perception that our models, platform, and applications contribute to job displacement or economic disruption could generate significant negative media coverage, customer or employee backlash, or organized opposition, regardless of the actual impact of our models, platform, and applications on employment. Our ability to maintain and enhance our brand and reputation also depends on the effectiveness and scalability of our internal communications and reputation-management capabilities, including our ability to support global operations, and limitations in these areas could adversely affect our brand as we grow. Our crisis response and reputation-management capabilities may be limited or underdeveloped, particularly as we scale, which could impair our ability to respond effectively to adverse events and mitigate reputational harm.

The promotion of our brand requires us to make substantial expenditures, which we anticipate will increase as our market becomes more competitive. We have in the past, and may in the future, face challenges in building broader brand awareness and may fall behind competitors with more established or widely recognized brands. Our brand promotion activities may not be successful or generate the awareness or increased revenue we expect, and even if they do, any increase in revenue may not offset the significant expenses we incur in the efforts to build our brand.

If we do not successfully maintain and enhance our brand, our business may not grow, we may have reduced pricing power relative to competitors, we could lose customers or fail to attract potential customers or we could fail to hire and retain employees, all of which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

We derive substantially all of our revenue from a limited number of products and applications, and our failure to maintain and expand our product portfolio could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

We currently derive substantially all of our revenue from a limited number of products and applications, primarily our Claude models and portfolio of applications that sit on top of the Claude Platform. This dependence on a narrower set of offerings subjects us to various risks that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Our concentration in a limited number of products and applications, as well as our focused development strategy within specific areas of the broader AI landscape, reduces our ability to mitigate business risks through diversification. While this focused approach may enable us to allocate resources efficiently and compete effectively in our prioritized areas, it also creates the risk that significant market opportunities or the largest addressable markets may emerge in areas outside our current strategic focus. Many of our competitors offer diverse product portfolios across multiple business lines or pursue broader development across AI modalities, enabling them to offset weaknesses in a particular area. In contrast, any decline in demand for our core offerings, whether due to competition, technological changes, pricing pressure, changes in customer demand, or other factors, would have a direct and disproportionate impact on our revenue and ability to achieve and sustain profitability. Our concentration also reflects a strategy that prioritizes model capability as a principal source of differentiation and relies substantially on organic adoption rather than a large direct sales organization. The risks of that strategy, including the risks that differentiation among advanced models narrows, that customers select providers based on factors other than capability, or that competitors capture the individual users from whom our enterprise adoption often originates, are described in the sections titled “—We face intense competition, and if we are unable to compete effectively, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected” and “—We have a limited history under our current go-to- market strategy, and our efforts to scale and evolve our customer acquisition, engagement, and retention model may not be successful.”

The AI industry is rapidly evolving, and it is difficult to predict which modalities or applications will capture the most durable commercial value or how the competitive landscape will shift. If the market for AI models, platforms, and applications develops in ways that do not align with our chosen areas of focus, or if competitors are better positioned to address the areas where the market opportunity ultimately proves largest, our ability to sustain revenue growth and compete effectively could be adversely affected. If we experience a decline in demand for our core offerings, we may not be able to successfully expand our product and application portfolio, and even if we do, our new products and applications may not achieve market acceptance or generate sufficient revenue to offset any decline in our existing business. Further, technical issues, security incidents, regulatory restrictions, or reputational harm affecting our models, platform, and applications could have an immediate and disproportionate impact on our business due to our product concentration where alternative revenue streams may not be available to offset such impact.

The market for AI models, platforms, and applications is evolving quickly, and customer requirements and expectations are changing rapidly. Customers may increasingly demand specialized solutions or specialized integration and tooling that we do not currently offer and may not plan to offer, or may seek AI solutions embedded more deeply into enterprise workflows. Enterprises may prefer to develop their own AI capabilities internally or choose competitors that offer more comprehensive product suites. Moreover, because substantially all of our models and applications are built on top of our frontier models, any defect, vulnerability, safety issue, or performance degradation in an underlying frontier model could simultaneously affect multiple products and applications and customer deployments, amplifying the impact of any such issue across our business. If we fail to anticipate or respond effectively to these evolving customer needs, we may lose customers to competitors or fail to acquire new customers.

In addition, we currently compete, and may increasingly compete, with some of our own customers. As we expand our product portfolio or introduce new capabilities, our offerings may overlap with or directly compete with the models, platforms, and applications that our customers provide. This risk is particularly acute with software-as-a-service and other software companies, whose offerings may incorporate AI functionality similar to, or dependent on, the capabilities we provide.
