# Document Gallery

Twenty-five documents a knowledge worker actually has to produce, each one written out in full in this folder. Every entry says **what the document is for**, **what shape it takes**, and **which one here to open**: copy it, delete what does not apply, and you have a starting point rather than a blank page.

The companion to this page is [[Visualization Gallery]], which does the same for charts and diagrams.

![A shared table, mid-review](assets/meeting-table.jpeg)

*Most of these documents exist because a group of people has to agree on something.*

## Running a company

### The annual review

**For:** the full year. What shipped, what it cost, what broke, what was learned. Read by a board, a team, or your future self. **Shape:** executive summary first, then one section per operating area, then the misses stated as plainly as the wins. Long-form prose carrying an image. **Open:** [[Annual Operating Review]]

### The quarterly business review

**For:** a narrower period and a sharper question. Usually "what changed, and what are we doing about it". **Shape:** one finding up front, evidence in charts and a metrics table, then actions with owners. Short enough to read in one sitting. **Open:** [[Quarterly Business Review]]

### The metrics dashboard

**For:** the numbers on their own, dense and dated, for someone who already knows the story and wants the current figures. **Shape:** KPI row, a chart per question, and the table the charts came from. **Open:** [[Revenue Dashboard]]

### The cost review

**For:** what something cost, itemised so nobody has to take it on faith. **Shape:** a waterfall of where spend went, an itemised table, the rates shown so the arithmetic can be checked, and an explicit list of what is *not* counted. **Open:** [[Cost of This Quarter]]

### The operating handbook

**For:** how a team works. The rhythms, the rules, the things a new joiner would otherwise learn by getting them wrong. **Shape:** short sections, heavy on tables, written to be skimmed and returned to. **Open:** [[Operating Notes — How We Work]]

## Deciding things

### The decision record

**For:** one decision, captured while it is fresh: what was decided, by whom, on what evidence, and what the disagreement was. **Shape:** decision and approvers at the top, the framing, an evidence table that includes the *weak* evidence, and the condition that got agreement. **Open:** [[How We Decided]]

### The investment memo

**For:** diligence on a company. The case for, the case against, and what you would need to believe. **Shape:** thesis, market, product, team, terms, risks. Tables for anything comparable; prose for judgment. **Open:** [[Investment Memo — Larkspur Robotics]]

### The market landscape

**For:** where you sit and who else is there, when the answer is not obvious. **Shape:** a diagram of the segmentation, a side-by-side comparison table, and a clearly-labelled hypothesis where the evidence runs out. **Open:** [[Market Landscape]]

### The technical evaluation

**For:** a bake-off. Several options, one recommendation, the losing options kept in so the reasoning survives. **Shape:** criteria stated before results, a results table, and a recommendation that names what would change it. **Open:** [[Runtime Evaluation — Local Models]]

## Talking to customers

### The research synthesis

**For:** turning many conversations into something a team can act on. **Shape:** method and sample first (including its limits), themes as a table with counts, verbatim quotes, and an explicit "what we are *not* concluding". **Open:** [[Customer Interviews]]

### Field notes

**For:** the raw material. What you saw and heard, before it is tidied into findings. **Shape:** chronological, lightly organised, honest about what is observation and what is inference. **Open:** [[Field Notes — Customer Visits]]

### The client proposal

**For:** scope, approach, price, and what happens next. **Shape:** the client's problem in their words first, then approach, timeline, team, and commercials. Every number a table. **Open:** [[Client Proposal — Meridian Retail]]

## Making things public

### The content plan

**For:** a quarter of publishing. What, when, for whom, and why each piece exists. **Shape:** a calendar table, a piece-by-piece breakdown, and the measure of whether it worked. **Open:** [[Content Strategy — Q4]]

### The press release

**For:** an announcement, written to be quoted. **Shape:** headline, dateline, the news in the first paragraph, a quote, then detail in descending importance. Visibly a draft until it is not. **Open:** [[Press Release Draft]]

### The product brief

**For:** what you are building, for whom, and how you will know it worked. **Shape:** problem, user, proposal, non-goals, risks, measures. Non-goals are the section that earns its place. **Open:** [[Product Brief — Reading Room]]

## Planning and running the work

### The project plan

**For:** a piece of work with a deadline, several owners, and at least one thing that can go wrong. **Shape:** what done looks like as measurable outcomes, a phase diagram, a Gantt of the schedule, a workstream table with owners, and the risks named rather than implied. **Open:** [[Project Plan — Website Relaunch]]

### Meeting notes

**For:** the record of what a group decided, written so someone absent can act on it. **Shape:** attendance, decisions as a table, actions as checkboxes with owners and dates, round-the-table, and an explicit parked list. **Open:** [[Meeting Notes — Weekly Staff]]

### The performance review

**For:** a written record of a conversation that has already happened. **Shape:** expectations set earlier scored against outcomes, what went well, what to work on stated concretely, the support owed in return, and the reviewee's own comments left in their words. **Open:** [[Performance Review — Mid-Year]]

## Reporting numbers

### The sales report

**For:** what closed, what did not, and whether next quarter is covered. **Shape:** attainment against quota per rep, a funnel table with conversion by stage, sources of business, and a weighted pipeline that states the gap plainly. **Open:** [[Sales Report — Q3]]

### The marketing dashboard

**For:** channel performance, dense enough to act on. **Shape:** a KPI row, traffic over time, a channel table carrying spend and return side by side, and what it implies for next quarter's budget. **Open:** [[Marketing Dashboard — Q3]]

### The annual report

**For:** shareholders, once a year. The numbers and the judgment behind them. **Shape:** three-year table, what worked, what did not at the same level of detail, and next year's commitments as a numbered list. **Open:** [[Annual Report — Shareholder Letter]]

## Personal

### The resume

**For:** your own record, kept current rather than rewritten in a panic. **Shape:** roles with outcomes rather than duties, a selected-work table, and a short section on how you actually work. **Open:** [[Resume — Priya Raman]]

### The trip planner

**For:** a trip with bookings, a budget, and things you will forget. **Shape:** a route diagram, a booking table, a budget chart, and checklists per stop, plus what you learned last time. **Open:** [[Trip Planner — Portugal, October]]

## Reference

### The chart catalogue

### The reference card

## Using these as templates

None of these are special. They are ordinary Markdown files, so:

- **Duplicate one** and edit over it: the structure is the value, not the words.
- **Keep the tables.** Most of what makes these readable is that comparable things sit in a grid instead of a paragraph.
- **Delete the sections that do not apply.** A short honest document beats a long one with empty headings.
- **Keep the "what we are not concluding" habit.** Several of these documents state their own limits, and that is what makes the rest of them trustworthy.
