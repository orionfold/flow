# Our Governance Structure and Its Goals

Our novel capital and corporate governance structure has been carefully designed and differs significantly from that of other public companies. We strive to help bring humanity safely into an era where transformative AI exerts tremendous and potentially decisive influence over a broad range of human undertakings. Operating as a PBC gives our board of directors the necessary latitude to balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and our Company Purpose. In addition, a financially disinterested LTBT, which generally has the power to elect and remove a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors, is designed to supply both the incentives and insights for striking this balance appropriately. We also believe that our seven co-founders are distinctly equipped to be stewards of our mission.

## Public Benefit Corporation

We are incorporated as a PBC under Delaware law, a structure that enables our commitment to AI safety and responsibility. PBCs are a class of corporations that are intended to produce a public benefit and to operate in a responsible and sustainable manner. As a PBC, we are required to balance (i) the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, (ii) the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and (iii) the Company Purpose. This requires our board of directors to weigh multiple factors, including relevant long- and short-term externalities of decisions, alongside the pecuniary interests of our stockholders. To date, we have found that the safety and societal benefit of AI and consideration of the best interests of all key stakeholder groups have generally aligned with our long-term commercial success and stockholders’ long-term monetary interests. However, as a PBC, we may make decisions that we believe appropriately balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose, but that negatively impact the short-, medium-, or long-term performance of our business or the market price of our Class A common stock. There is limited judicial precedent interpreting the balancing requirement applicable to PBCs under Delaware law, and there is therefore uncertainty as to how a court would view our directors’ balancing of these potentially competing interests. For a discussion of these risks, see “Risk Factors—Our focus on the long-term best interests of our company as a PBC, and the duty of our directors to balance the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those stakeholders materially affected by our conduct, and our Company Purpose, may conflict with short-, medium-, or long-term financial interests and business performance, which may negatively impact the value of our Class A common stock” and “Risk Factors—As a Delaware public benefit corporation, we may be subject to increased litigation concerning our board of directors’ obligation to balance our stockholders’ pecuniary interests, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct and our Company Purpose, the occurrence of which may have an adverse impact on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.”

In measuring our public benefit performance, our board of directors will consider the objectives set forth in our five mission pillars: that the technical problems of AI safety are solved, that AI safety is a universal practice backstopped by law, that AI strengthens democracy and global stability, that AI expands prosperity and human flourishing, and that AI’s benefits are shared by everyone. We may also address the additional interests of our customers, partners, employees, and other stakeholder constituencies that we may identify from time to time. Neither our amended and restated certificate of incorporation nor our amended and restated bylaws will require that we measure our public benefit performance against an objective third-party standard, and our board of directors will be responsible for establishing and overseeing the standards by which to measure our public benefit performance against these objectives. We currently expect that our objectives and standards will be qualitative in nature and may evolve over time as our business grows and as our understanding of our public benefit impact develops.

As required by Section 366(b) of the DGCL, we will provide our stockholders no less than biennially with a statement assessing our promotion of the Company Purpose and of the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct. The statement will set out our objectives and standards and describe the actions we have taken. We intend to make the statement available to stockholders and the public through our website. We also expect to demonstrate our progress towards these goals by continuing to publish evidence-based research on our website regarding our work and its impact.

## Long-Term Benefit Trust

We created the LTBT, which was designed to ensure that we are managed in a manner that responsibly balances our tripartite goals as a PBC: (i) the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, (ii) the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and (iii) the Company Purpose. The LTBT is organized as a common law “noncharitable purpose trust” under Delaware law and governed by the Long-Term Benefit Trust Amended and Restated Trust Agreement (the “Trust Agreement”). The LTBT is intended to operate as an independent body of Trustees with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy, and social enterprise. The LTBT’s arrangements are designed to minimize the LTBT’s and the Trustees’ exposure to our financial performance and grant the Trustees sufficient independence to ensure we are striking this balance appropriately.

Through a special class of common stock, Class T common stock, the LTBT generally has the authority to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. Class T common stock has no economic rights.

Decisions regarding our business and affairs are made by our board of directors. While the LTBT has significant influence over our board of directors through its ability to generally elect and remove Class T directors, neither the LTBT nor the Trustees have decision-making authority in the day-to-day management or strategy setting of the company. The LTBT’s role is to select directors who are well-equipped to responsibly balance our tripartite goals as a PBC, and to support our management’s decision-making through advice and guidance. Certain actions, such as a merger, consolidation, or sale of all or substantially all of our assets, will require the approval of our board of directors pursuant to Delaware law, a majority of whom are elected by the LTBT.

### Election and Removal of Directors

As the sole holder of Class T common stock, the LTBT generally has the authority to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors. Our Class T common stock will not vote on the election or removal of directors elected by the holders of our Class A common stock and Class F common stock, who will be elected or removed solely by the holders of Class A common stock and Class F common stock, voting together. For a discussion of the members of our board of directors who are currently Class T directors and those that will be Class T directors upon the completion of this offering, see the section titled “Management—Board of Directors.”

Prior to nominating or removing a director, the nominating party must provide at least 30 days’ notice to our NGC, which notice period is waivable by our NGC in its discretion. Following the completion of this offering, our NGC will be composed of one Class T director and two directors who are not Class T directors and will act by majority vote.

With respect to one of the Class T director seats (the “Specified Class T Director”), the LTBT’s ability to nominate that director, to remove that director without cause, or to change the director designated as the Specified Class T Director is subject to the concurrence of our NGC. If our NGC does not concur with such nomination, removal, or change in designation within 15 days of receiving notice, the matter will be submitted to a vote of the holders of our Class E common stock unless the LTBT elects to withdraw the notice. If submitted to a vote of the holders of Class E common stock, approval of the LTBT’s proposal will require a majority of the votes cast by our Class E common stock (subject to a quorum of a majority of our outstanding Class E common stock). The LTBT may withdraw any notice of nomination, removal, or change in designation at any time before the taking of such a vote. Any Class T director, including the Specified Class T Director, may be removed for cause, which will be determined in accordance with Delaware law, without the concurrence of our NGC or the approval of the holders of our Class E common stock; provided, however, that each of the LTBT and the Founder LLC has agreed to provide 30 calendar days’ notice to the other stockholder and the NGC prior to effecting a removal of any director for cause.

### Trustees of the LTBT

The LTBT is governed by five Trustees. The Trustees have the authority to appoint the Trustees by majority vote of the Trustees then in office and to remove the Trustees by majority vote of the total number of authorized Trustees or by the unanimous approval of the other Trustees then in office and the approval of the NGC, provided that the LTBT must provide at least 30 days’ prior written notice to our NGC before effecting any such appointment or removal. The notice period is waivable by our NGC in its discretion. Trustees may also be removed by order of the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware upon petition by a person designated by the Trust Agreement as having the power to enforce the agreement if a Trustee has engaged in disqualifying conduct and removal would best serve the purpose of the LTBT. Trustees will serve annual terms, and there is no limit to the number of terms a Trustee may serve. Trustees are selected based on their expertise in areas relevant to the LTBT’s purpose, including AI safety, national security, public policy, social enterprise, and related fields. In practice to date, the Trustees and company executives have collaborated closely on sourcing, interviewing, and selecting Trustees. Of the five authorized seats for Trustees, one is currently vacant.

We believe that our success depends in part on establishing a capital structure that enables our co-founders, management, and board of directors to focus on strategic initiatives that create lasting value for our stakeholders, rather than facing pressure to prioritize short-term results or purely pecuniary objectives at the expense of our long-term vision and mission. ## Board Governance

Decisions regarding our business and affairs are made by our board of directors. Immediately prior to the completion of this offering, we will enter into the Stockholders’ Agreement with the LTBT and the Founder LLC, under which they will agree to vote their shares and take other actions to give effect to the board composition, director nomination, and director removal provisions described in our governing documents. Collectively, the LTBT and the Founder LLC will have the ability to elect all members of our board of directors. Regardless of the class or classes of stock by which a director is elected, there is no limit to the number of terms a director may serve.

While the LTBT has significant influence over our board of directors through its ability to generally elect and remove a majority of the authorized members of our board of directors, neither the LTBT nor the Trustees have decision-making authority in the day-to-day management or strategy setting of the company. The LTBT’s role is to select directors who are well-equipped to make decisions in furtherance of the responsible balancing of the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose, and to support our management’s decision-making through advice and guidance. The Trustees aim to choose directors who have the necessary business skills, experience, and expertise to oversee the management of our business effectively and in a manner that responsibly balances (i) the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, (ii) the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and (iii) the Company Purpose.
