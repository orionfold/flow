#### Competition, Customers, and Market Adoption

We face intense competition, and if we are unable to compete effectively, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.

The market for AI models, platforms, and applications is rapidly evolving, intensely competitive, and subject to frequent and significant changes in technology, evolving customer requirements and expectations, shifting industry standards, regulatory developments, new market entrants, and new models, platforms, and applications. We face competition from a wide range of companies across multiple categories.

We compete directly with companies developing frontier models for third-party software developers and enterprises. Our primary competitors in this category include Alphabet Inc. (together with its affiliates, “Google”) and OpenAI Group PBC (“OpenAI”). These competitors are developing increasingly capable AI systems and compete with us for customers, talent, compute, and strategic partnerships. Many have substantially greater financial, technical, and marketing resources than we do, their own or greater access to sources of compute, longer operating histories, greater brand recognition, and established relationships with current and potential customers and partners, which facilitates their ability to integrate their models into first- party applications sold directly to businesses.

We also compete with a broader set of model providers operating at or below the frontier, including large companies, specialized AI labs, and a growing range of other open-weight or open-source (shared weights, training data, and code) models released by international and domestic organizations. Such providers compete for a growing range of enterprise workloads that prioritize cost efficiency over intelligence. In particular, certain large companies compete through proprietary AI models, integrated AI capabilities in existing products, and control of cloud infrastructure and distribution channels. Some of these companies also have substantially greater financial, technical, and marketing resources than we do, their own or greater access to sources of compute, longer operating histories, greater brand recognition, and established relationships with current and potential customers and partners. In addition, some have substantially broader and more diverse product and service offerings and may be able to leverage existing relationships with distribution partners and customers to gain business in ways that discourage customers from purchasing our models, platform, and applications. For example, competitors may bundle AI capabilities with other products, integrate AI functionality into products that customers already use, offer closed technology platforms that lock customers into their ecosystems, sell products at zero or negative margins to gain market share, or use their substantial financial resources to subsidize the cost of AI models, platforms, and applications. Customers may prefer to purchase from existing vendors with which they have established relationships, even if our models, platform, and applications offer superior features or performance. Further, large companies that are currently our customers may choose to develop their own AI capabilities internally rather than purchase our models, platform, and applications.

Specialized AI labs, on the other hand, are competitive due to their ability to offer specific AI applications and differentiated business models. These companies can move quickly, attract top talent, develop novel approaches, platforms, or business models that disrupt existing competitive dynamics, and introduce technologies or products with greater performance or functionality, lower cost, ease of implementation, or other advantages. Some competitors or customers may have access to proprietary datasets, distribution channels, or domain-specific expertise that we do not possess, or new competitors may emerge that address particular industries or use cases more effectively than our models, platform, and applications, which could enable them to develop more specialized or higher-performing AI systems. In addition, competitors, customers, or other third parties have attempted in the past and will likely continue to attempt to replicate, distill, extract, or otherwise derive the functionality or capabilities of our models through permitted or impermissible means, including through our research previews. These actions could reduce the technological differentiation of our offerings; enable the development of competing products at lower cost, with reduced research and development investment, or that replicate our models without safety controls or safeguards that were incorporated in our versions; implicate national security concerns or strain geopolitical tensions; and intensify competitive pressures. In addition, efforts to prevent or mitigate model replication, distillation, or other forms of extraction

may require us to modify our models, platform, and applications, limit features or use cases, or implement technical safeguards that could reduce the usability or attractiveness of our offerings, slow adoption, and adversely affect customer demand and revenue. More broadly, as AI models are increasingly used to develop other AI models, the knowledge and techniques required to build frontier models may become more widely available over time, including through distillation, unauthorized access to or theft of model weights, or the diffusion of AI-assisted research and development capabilities across the industry. If the ability to develop highly capable models becomes more broadly available, our technological differentiation could erode and competition could increasingly depend on access to large-scale compute rather than on model development capability. In such an environment, access to sufficient compute on economical terms could become the primary constraint on our ability to train and serve competitive models, and our business, competitive position, and results of operations could be adversely affected if we are unable to secure compute at the scale, cost, or on the timeline we require.

Additionally, open-weight or open-source AI models may capture a portion of the market for certain use cases, which could exert pricing pressure on our offerings or lead certain customers to adopt open-source alternatives for those use cases.

A significant portion of our customer base consists of startups and technology-oriented enterprises that may be more sensitive to adverse macroeconomic conditions and may reduce or delay spending during a downturn. Because our cost structure limits our ability to rapidly adjust spending, a sustained economic downturn could disproportionately disadvantage us relative to larger, better-capitalized competitors. Such competitors may be better positioned to absorb or withstand adverse economic conditions or reductions in customer technology spending due to their greater financial resources, diversified product offerings and customer bases, and more stable revenue streams. It could also disadvantage us relative to smaller startups with leaner cost structures, lower fixed overhead, and fewer long-term contractual commitments that could help enable them to adjust spending more rapidly than we can. As a result, competitive pressures on our business could intensify, which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

If we are unable to differentiate our models, platform, and applications from competing offerings through enhanced functionality, superior performance, better safety features and reliability, lower cost, or other factors, demand for our models, platform, and applications may decline. The rapid pace of technological change in AI means that models, platforms, and applications can quickly become outdated. We must continuously invest in research and development to maintain our competitive position, but there is no guarantee that our investments will result in models, platforms, and applications that customers prefer over competing alternatives. Moreover, we are first and foremost a research lab, and our strategy prioritizes frontier research and model capability. We believe the advancing intelligence and capabilities of our models are the principal drivers of their adoption, and we have historically prioritized research and model development rather than new products and applications or sales and marketing. This approach assumes that model capability will continue to be a meaningful source of competitive differentiation. However, as the AI industry matures, the degree of differentiation among advanced AI models may narrow. This convergence may result from factors such as overlapping or similarly sourced training data, the widespread adoption of comparable model architectures, training techniques, and optimization methods, shared access to computing infrastructure, and the rapid dissemination of research advances across the industry. To the extent competitors are able to achieve comparable model capabilities with lower development or deployment costs, our ability to differentiate our models, platform, and applications on the basis of performance, functionality, or cost may be diminished. If differentiation among advanced models narrows, or if customers increasingly select AI providers on the basis of application features, user experience, distribution, integration into products they already use, or the strength of a sales organization rather than model capability, competitors that have invested more heavily than we have in those areas could be better positioned than we are. Responding to such a shift could require us to increase our investment in product development and sales and marketing substantially beyond our current plans, which could take longer or cost more than we anticipate and could reduce the compute and other resources available for frontier research, and we may not be able to execute such a shift quickly or effectively. See also the section titled “—We have a limited history under our current go-to-market strategy, and our efforts to scale and evolve our customer acquisition, engagement, and retention model may not be successful.”

If we are unable to compete successfully, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.

If we fail to attract new customers, retain existing customers, or expand usage of our models, platform, and applications, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.

Our future growth depends on our ability to attract new customers, retain existing customers, and expand sales within our existing customer base. We face significant challenges in each of these areas, and failure to execute on our customer acquisition and retention strategies could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

We must continue to attract new customers in a cost-effective manner. Potential customers may not understand the benefits of our models, platform, and applications or may be unable to justify the investment in our models, platform, and applications, particularly as existing competitors continue to enhance their offerings, improve performance, expand functionality, or reduce costs. Our ability to demonstrate differentiated value to potential customers may become more difficult as this competition intensifies, or as large technology companies bundle AI capabilities into existing products. If we continue to focus on enterprise customers, we may face longer sales cycles that demand significant upfront investment in enterprise sales teams and customer service. These sales efforts are costly and may not result in customer conversion.

Even if we successfully attract new customers, we face significant challenges in retaining them. Many of our customer contracts allow customers to reduce usage or terminate with limited or no notice. Customers are not obligated to renew their subscriptions or contracts with us. Customer retention and usage levels may decline due to a number of factors, including:

•dissatisfaction with the performance, reliability, safety, or accuracy of our AI models;

•availability of competitive alternatives;

•customers developing their own AI capabilities internally;

•our models, platform, and applications reducing demand for our customers’ offerings or causing

disruptions to their businesses, which could lead to reduced usage or customer attrition;

•changes in customer budgets or strategic priorities regarding AI investment;

•concerns about data privacy, security, or our data handling practices;

•negative publicity regarding AI generally or our business specifically;

•changes in the regulatory and legal landscape surrounding AI and our models, platform, and

applications in particular;

•pricing changes or perception that our pricing is not competitive; and

•general economic conditions or industry-specific downturns affecting our customers.

Our ability to retain customers and drive expansion also depends on our continued investment in product development. The process of developing products and incorporating new technologies is complex and uncertain, and if we fail to accurately predict customers’ changing needs and emerging technological trends and opportunities, we may not be able to compete effectively and our business and ability to generate revenue could be adversely affected. As AI technology evolves rapidly, customers may perceive our models, platform, and applications as becoming outdated or may expect continuous improvements to model performance and capabilities. Even minor degradations in model performance or increases in response latency could cause customers to reduce usage or switch to competitors. If we are unable to retain existing customers or if our customers do not maintain their usage levels, our revenue and growth could be adversely affected. For additional discussion on product development, see the section titled “—Our success depends in large part on our ability to continue to develop the most advanced and capable AI models, and any failure to do so could adversely affect our business.”

Some of our enterprise customers may have the financial resources, technical capabilities, and access to the talent and compute necessary to develop and deploy proprietary AI models, and may reduce or cease usage of

our models, platform, and applications as their internal AI capabilities mature, even where they initially adopted our offerings as a foundation for their AI strategy. If these customers were to reduce or cease usage of our models, platform, and applications, our revenue and growth could be adversely affected.

In addition to retaining customers, our growth strategy depends in part on expanding usage within our existing customer base by increasing usage volumes, selling additional capabilities, and expanding adoption across departments and use cases within customer organizations. However, customers may not expand their usage for a variety of reasons, including satisfaction with current usage levels, budget constraints, technical challenges in integrating our models, platform, and applications more broadly within their organizations, or preference for multi-vendor AI strategies in order to limit dependence on any single provider. Due to our consumption-based pricing model, customers may reduce or cease usage at any point within a given period due to budget or internal procurement constraints, organizational spending controls, or other factors unrelated to the quality or performance of our models, platform, and applications, and may not resume usage until a new budget period commences or additional spend is approved, even where demand for our models, platform, and applications remains strong. As a result, our revenue could be lower or more variable than anticipated and this effect may be particularly pronounced among customers experiencing rapid growth in their usage of AI. If we increase sales and marketing expenditures to accelerate customer acquisition without achieving proportional revenue growth and if we cannot attract new customers, retain existing customers, expand within our customer base, or accomplish these objectives in a cost-effective manner or timely manner, our revenue growth will decline and our business, financial condition, and results of operations will be adversely affected.

If the market for our models, platform, and applications develops more slowly than we expect or declines, our growth may slow or stall, and our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.

The market for AI technologies, including frontier AI and related models, platforms, and applications, has grown rapidly in recent years, but is still relatively nascent and evolving rapidly, and it is uncertain whether the market for such AI technologies will achieve or sustain widespread adoption at the pace or to the extent we expect. Our ability to generate revenue and achieve sustainable growth depends in significant part on the extent to which enterprises, software developers, and other users adopt AI technologies, integrate them into existing workflows, expand usage over time, and are willing to pay for our models, platform, and applications at levels that support durable economic value at scale. Market adoption may be slower than anticipated due to factors such as uncertainty regarding the cost, performance, reliability, and perceived value of AI solutions; increased regulation; concerns about security, privacy, legal and regulatory compliance, or ethical considerations; technological challenges; level of effort and expense associated with integration efforts; competition from alternative or emerging technologies; the availability or adoption of complementary infrastructure and workflows; or reductions in corporate spending due to macroeconomic conditions. Moreover, demand for frontier models may decline if customers determine that less advanced, lower-cost models are sufficient for their particular use cases. As AI capabilities improve across the industry and a broader range of models becomes available, customers may conclude that incremental performance gains at the frontier do not justify the additional cost, and may migrate toward smaller, cheaper, or open-source alternatives that adequately address their needs. If we are unable to demonstrate sufficient differentiated value from our frontier models relative to less capable alternatives, our revenue and growth could be adversely affected, even in a market where overall AI adoption continues to grow. Further, while AI adoption may continue or accelerate in certain areas, the long-term trajectory of this technological trend is uncertain, and market understanding, acceptance, and valuation of AI-enabled models, platforms, and applications may be inaccurate or change over time and adoption may occur unevenly across industries, geographies, and customer types. If growth in the AI market does not translate into sustained customer demand for our offerings, expanded usage, and increased revenue, or if anticipated use cases fail to materialize, our prospects, business, financial condition, and results of operations could be adversely affected.
