# Executive Compensation

## Compensation Discussion and Analysis

The purpose of this Compensation Discussion and Analysis (“CD&A”) section is to discuss our compensation philosophy, our executive compensation program, and the compensation for our named executive officers (“NEOs”).

Our NEOs for the year ended December 31, 2025 were:

•Dario Amodei, our Chief Executive Officer, Co-Founder, and Director;

•Daniela Amodei, our President, Co-Founder, and Director; and

•Krishna Rao, our Chief Financial Officer.

This discussion contains forward-looking statements that are based on our current plans, considerations, expectations, and determinations regarding future compensation programs. See also the section titled “Special Note Regarding Forward-looking Statements.” Actual compensation programs that we adopt in the future may differ materially from the currently planned programs summarized in this discussion. For additional information regarding the compensation programs for, and compensation provided to, our NEOs, see the section titled “—Post-Fiscal Year End Compensation Changes and Equity Awards.”

## Executive Summary

At Anthropic, we strive to build a team that is aligned with our mission and values. Executive compensation reflects our overall compensation philosophy, which prioritizes competitive pay that targets the 90th percentile of the AI development market, consistent pay for the same role, level, and location, and simplicity of plan design. In 2025, we offered a competitive mix of base salary, long-term equity incentive awards (including stock options and RSUs), and other benefits to attract and retain top talent in a competitive market environment.

### Compensation Philosophy and Objectives

Our executive compensation philosophy is to provide our executive officers with consistent cash and equity compensation for the same role, level, and location. When determining executive compensation, we review each executive’s current compensation against role- and level-specific market data. We do this bi-annually as part of our performance and compensation cycle where we review proposed market-based and promotion-based cash and equity increases for executives. We plan to continue to regularly review, and potentially adjust, our executive compensation programs to ensure they are consistent with our compensation philosophy as a public company.

In determining compensation for our senior leaders, we distinguish between our seven co-founders (among whom only Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei are executive officers) and our other senior leaders who are not co- founders (among whom only Mr. Rao is an executive officer). Each co-founder is paid equal compensation. As a non-co-founder senior leader, Mr. Rao received base salary and equity compensation based on market data for his role and level.

Our executive compensation program is structured to achieve the objectives described below. We regularly review our executive compensation program to assess whether it meets these objectives and adapts to evolving economic conditions.

•Competitive Pay to Attract and Retain Top Talent. We provide our executives with competitive cash

and equity pay for each role, location, and level to attract mission-aligned, top executive talent.

•Internal Parity. We take internal parity into account when determining executive compensation. As

co-founders, Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei are awarded equal compensation, including any annual cash or equity increases. As a non-co-founder senior leader, Mr. Rao is compensated based on the market

265

data from our peer group for his role, level, and location. We review market data twice a year and make adjustments to our compensation benchmarks as needed.

•Aligning Executive Interests with Our Stockholders. We provide incentives that seek to align our

executives’ interests with those of our stockholders through long-term equity awards, which vest over time.

### Executive Compensation Process

#### Role of the Board of Directors and Compensation Committee

Our executive compensation program has historically been overseen by the non-employee members of our board of directors, which, as a group, has made decisions regarding the compensation for our NEOs after considering recommendations from management, as further discussed below.

Following the completion of this offering, the compensation committee of our board of directors will oversee our executive compensation program and provide recommendations to our board of directors on compensation decisions for our executive officers. The compensation committee will work closely with its independent compensation consultant (engaged in connection with this offering, as described below) and meet regularly to make decisions on our executive compensation program. We expect that our compensation committee will review a variety of market data and information, including survey data, and consider the recommendations of its independent compensation consultant when making compensation decisions. Following the completion of this offering, the compensation committee’s responsibilities will include, among other things, reviewing and approving (or making recommendations to our board of directors, as applicable): overall compensation strategy; amounts and form of executive compensation; goals and objectives to be considered in determining the compensation of our Chief Executive Officer and other executive officers; annual and long-term incentive plans and benefit plans; board of directors compensation; whether such compensation policies and practices create risks that are reasonably likely to have a material adverse effect on the company; annual say-on-pay vote results; annual proxy and CD&A disclosure; and compensation peer group.

#### Role of Management

Our Chief Executive Officer and our other executive officers do not set their own compensation, nor are they present when our board of directors sets their individual compensation. Our President reviews and advises on compensation for the seven co-founders but does not make specific recommendations on or participate in decisions related to her compensation or our Chief Executive Officer’s compensation. Our President also provides her evaluation and makes recommendations with respect to base salary and equity awards for each

266

other executive officer, which are considered by our board of directors as it ultimately makes its determinations. Our people team provides further analysis and feedback as requested by our board of directors with regard to the compensation of our executives.

#### Role of Independent Compensation Consultant

In connection with this offering, we have engaged Semler Brossy Consulting Group LLC as an independent compensation consultant to formalize our post-offering compensation programs and implement compensation arrangements that reflect our compensation philosophy described above.

### Base Salary

Our board of directors generally reviews the base salaries of our NEOs twice a year. The factors used to determine base salary for our executives include market data for the role, level, and location, as well as internal parity (which, for our co-founder NEOs, includes considerations related to parity with our technical leadership).

From January 1, 2025 to June 15, 2025, the annual base salary for each of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei was $600,000. Effective June 16, 2025, the annual base salary for each of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei was increased to $700,000. The increase in the executives’ cash compensation was intended to ensure competitive and equitable compensation for our leaders. In particular, the salary increases recognized their ongoing leadership roles, while considering relative parity across executives, including our technical leadership.

From January 1, 2025 to December 15, 2025, the annual base salary for Mr. Rao was $700,000, which was increased to $850,000 effective December 16, 2025. The salary increase was based on market data for Mr. Rao’s role and level, as well as internal parity for non-co-founder senior leaders who are not executive officers.

### Long-Term Equity Incentive Program

Each year, our NEOs are eligible to receive equity awards under our 2021 Plan. By structuring the majority of our NEOs’ target total direct compensation payable in the form of equity that vests over a number of years, we create incentives for our NEOs to remain employed with Anthropic and pursue actions that will benefit our company and stockholders over the long term.

On June 12, 2025, our board of directors granted 992,500 options (“2025 Options”) and 1,717,000 RSUs (“2025 RSUs”) to each of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei, after carefully considering several factors, including each executive’s experience, skills, responsibilities, and impact to the company; individual and company performance; and internal parity.

The 2025 Options vested in equal monthly installments over a one-year period beginning on January 1, 2025, subject to the executive’s continuous service with the company through each vest date. The 2025 Options were granted with an exercise price equal to $2.38 per share. The 2025 RSUs vest on the first date upon which both a service-based condition and a performance-based condition are satisfied. The service-based condition is satisfied as to 1/12th of the total 2025 RSUs on February 20, 2026, and on each quarterly anniversary thereafter, subject to the executive’s continuous service with the company through each vest date. The performance-based vesting condition of the 2025 RSUs will be satisfied upon the effectiveness of the registration statement of which this prospectus forms a part.

### Perquisites and Other Executive Benefits

We generally provide the same benefits to all of our employees, including our NEOs, and generally do not maintain benefit plans that cover only executive officers or provide perquisites or personal benefits to our NEOs. However, we do provide certain travel and security benefits to some of our NEOs. These benefits are described in more detail below. Our board of directors believes these benefits are reasonable and necessary, and for the benefit of our company. These benefits are intended to promote the productivity of our executives, in addition to ensuring their personal health, safety, and security. The aggregate incremental cost of providing these benefits to our NEOs is disclosed in the “All Other Compensation” column of the Summary Compensation Table.

#### Travel

We maintain a Travel and Expense Policy (the “Travel Policy”), which outlines guidelines and allowances for executive travel and expenses, recognizing the needs and responsibilities of our senior leadership team. The Travel Policy governs all business-related travel and expenses for our executives, except to the extent benefits are provided through a security program authorized and/or approved by our board of directors. Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei are entitled to certain enhanced travel benefits recommended by the security studies on which their security program is based (discussed in the section below titled “Security Services”). The Travel Policy is reviewed annually by our management team to ensure it remains appropriate and aligned with company objectives and industry standards. To the extent required by the relevant tax laws, the value of any personal use, including any personal elements of business trips, will be treated as taxable compensation to the executive, and

#### Security Services

The protection of our senior leadership is a critical priority for our company and our stockholders. In connection with our security program and our evaluation of security threats, we offer security services to certain of our NEOs and may provide security services to other NEOs in the future. Such security services may include: (i) individual protection services (including the protection of family members) and home security systems and monitoring, (ii) private aviation, and (iii) private car and driver services. These safeguards are implemented due to the variety of business-related security concerns faced by our senior leaders, and they are comparable to those provided to senior leaders of comparable high-profile companies in the technology industry.

In 2025, a leading security consulting firm conducted independent, third-party security studies regarding the personal and security risks of our Chief Executive Officer and our President. Based on these security studies, increased media attention for Anthropic, and the identification of bona fide business-related, heightened security concerns arising as a result of the respective positions of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei within the company, our board of directors determined that enhanced personal security services were necessary to increase the safety and protection of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei, and to avoid an impact to our business continuity if a security incident occurred. Our board of directors regularly assesses security concerns and reviews the security services provided to Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei and the cost of such services in light of such concerns, and other benefits to our company. The security measures for Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei in 2025 included (i) individual protection services (including during personal travel), which included the protection of family members, as well as enhanced home security systems and surveillance, (ii) private car and driver services for ground transportation, and (iii) in the case of Dr. Amodei, the use of private aircraft for both business and personal travel.

Given the importance of the roles of Dr. Amodei and Ms. Amodei at Anthropic, our board of directors required the security measures described above for the benefit of our company, and we view the costs of these services as necessary and appropriate. In the “All Other Compensation” column of the Summary Compensation Table, we have disclosed the aggregate incremental cost of the security benefits that may be deemed to be perquisites under current relevant disclosure rules. We did not pay expenses for personal security for any other NEOs in 2025, except in connection with business-related travel.

## Termination and Change of Control Benefits

The employment of our NEOs is “at-will,” meaning we can terminate them at any time, and they can terminate

their employment with us at any time. Other than Mr. Rao, our NEOs were not entitled to any severance or

change of control benefits during 2025.

During 2025, the Rao Offer Letter provided that if, at any time, his employment was terminated by the company without “cause” or he resigned for “good reason” (as such terms were defined in the Rao Offer Letter), then, subject to his execution of a release of claims in favor of the company and other conditions, he would be entitled to the following severance payments and benefits: (i) an amount equal to six months of his then-current base salary, (ii) payment of monthly premiums for coverage under the Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (“COBRA”) for up to six months, and (iii) accelerated vesting as to 1/8th of the Rao Options.

The Rao Offer Letter further provided that if, within 30 days prior to, upon, or at any time following a “change of control” (as defined in our 2021 Plan), his employment was terminated by the company without cause or he resigned for good reason, then, subject to his execution of a release of claims in favor of the company and other conditions, 100% of any then unvested shares subject to his then outstanding equity awards, including the Rao Options, would accelerate and vest. If, in the change of control, his then-outstanding equity awards would otherwise have been terminated for no consideration at the closing of the change of control event, then those awards would instead have become fully vested as of immediately prior to such closing. Upon the termination of the Rao Offer Letter in October 2026, we entered into a letter agreement with Mr. Rao continuing the change of control vesting acceleration benefit described herein on the same terms, without alteration.

For additional information regarding the severance and change of control benefits for our NEOs, see the sections titled “—Post-Fiscal Year End Compensation Changes and Equity Awards” and “—Potential Payments upon Termination or Change of Control.”

## Other Compensation Governance Practices

### Compensation Recovery Policy

We have adopted an executive compensation recoupment policy intended to comply with the requirements of Rule 10D-1 under the Exchange Act and the rules of the stock exchange on which our securities are listed (“Clawback Policy”) that will be effective upon the effectiveness of the registration statement of which this prospectus forms a part. The Clawback Policy will generally require us to recover erroneously awarded incentive-based compensation that was received by covered executive officers in the event we are required to prepare an accounting restatement due to any material noncompliance with any financial reporting requirement under the securities laws (including any required accounting restatement to correct an error in previously issued financial statements that is material to the previously issued financial statements), or that would result in a material misstatement if the error were corrected in the current period or left uncorrected in the current period. The Clawback Policy generally covers incentive-based compensation received on or after the effective date of the Clawback Policy and during the three-year period prior to the date we are required to prepare the financial restatement. The amount required to be recovered is the excess of the amount of incentive-based compensation received over the amount that otherwise would have been received based on the restated financial measure.

### Hedging, Pledging, and Insider Trading

We are dedicated to fostering high standards of ethical business conduct and adherence to applicable laws, rules, and regulations. Accordingly, we maintain our Insider Trading Policy, which we believe is appropriately

270

designed to foster compliance with insider trading laws, rules, and regulations, and the listing standards of the Nasdaq Global Select Market that will be applicable to us. Our Insider Trading Policy applies to all of our officers, directors, and employees, as well as designated consultants, and specifically prohibits ownership of financial instruments or participation in investment strategies that hedge the economic risk of owning our equity securities, such as prepaid variable forwards, equity swaps, and collars. Directors, officers, and other employees are prohibited from maintaining company securities in a margin account or otherwise pledging company securities as collateral for a loan, provided that certain limited exceptions may be permitted. In addition, we prohibit our executive officers, directors, and employees from purchasing or selling our securities while in possession of material, non-public information or otherwise utilizing such information for their personal benefit. We maintain a quarterly blackout window where applicable individuals may not trade.

### Policies and Practices Related to the Grant of Certain Equity Awards Close in Time to the Release of Material Nonpublic Information

We periodically grant stock options to our NEOs, employees, and other service providers. We have historically granted options on or shortly after a newly hired employee’s start date. We have historically granted refresh, promotion, or market adjustment options when and as determined by our board of directors. We have no specific policy or practice regarding the timing of options or other equity grants in relation to the public disclosure of material nonpublic information by the company.

## Tax and Accounting Considerations

### Tax Considerations

Section 162(m) of the Code imposes a $1 million limit on the amount of compensation that we may deduct as a business expense in any year with regard to certain of our most highly paid executive officers. While our board of directors considers the deductibility of compensation as one factor in determining executive compensation, our board of directors retains the discretion to award compensation that is not deductible as it believes that it is in our best interests to maintain flexibility in our approach to executive compensation in order to design a program that we deem to be the most effective in attracting, motivating, and retaining key executives and aligning pay with performance.

### Accounting Considerations

Our board of directors takes accounting considerations into account in designing compensation plans and arrangements for our NEOs and other employees. We follow the Financial Accounting Standards Board’s Accounting Standards Codification Topic 718 (“FASB ASC Topic 718”) for our stock-based compensation awards. FASB ASC Topic 718 requires us to measure the compensation expense for all share-based payment awards based on the grant date “fair value” of these awards.

Employees may have to satisfy one or more of the following service requirements before participating in the 423 components of our ESPP, as determined by the administrator, including: (1) continuous service with us or one of our affiliates for at least two years (or such lesser period of time as may be determined by our board of directors), (2) being customarily employed for more than 20 hours per week (or such lesser period of time as may be determined by our board of directors), (3) being customarily employed for more than five months per calendar year (or such lesser period of time as may be determined by our board of directors), (4) is not a highly compensated employee within the meaning of Section 423(b)(4)(D) of the Code, or (5) such other criteria as our board of directors may determine to be consistent with Section 423 of the Code. No employee may purchase shares under the 423 component of our ESPP at a rate in excess of $25,000 worth of our Class A common stock based on the fair market value per share of our Class A common stock at the beginning of an offering for each year such a purchase right is outstanding. Finally, no employee will be eligible for the grant of any purchase rights under the 423 component of our ESPP if immediately after such rights are granted, such employee has voting power over 5% or more of our outstanding capital stock measured by vote or value under Section 424(d) of the Code.

Offerings. Our ESPP is implemented through offerings pursuant to which participants are granted rights to

purchase shares of our Class A common stock on specified dates during such offerings. Under our ESPP, we may specify offerings with durations of not more than 27 months and may specify shorter purchase periods within each offering. Each offering will have one or more purchase dates on which shares of our Class A common stock will be purchased by participants in the offering. An offering under our ESPP may be terminated under certain circumstances. The administrator will have the discretion to structure an offering so that if the fair market value of a share of our Class A common stock on the first trading day of a new purchase period within that offering is less than or equal to the fair market value of a share of our Class A common stock on the offering date for that offering, then that offering will terminate immediately as of that first trading day, and the participants in such terminated offering will be automatically enrolled in a new offering beginning on the first trading day of such new offering.

Payroll Deductions. Participants who are employees may generally make contributions through payroll

deductions (unless otherwise required by law or provided for in an offering). In the case of an offering under the 423 component, such percentage or maximum dollar amount of contributions may not exceed 15% of their

Certain Adjustments. In the event of any change that is made in, or other events that occur with respect to, our

Class A common stock subject to our ESPP or subject to any purchase right without the receipt of consideration by the company through merger, consolidation, reorganization, recapitalization, reincorporation, stock dividend, dividend in property other than cash, large nonrecurring cash dividend, stock split, liquidating dividend, combination of shares, exchange of shares, change in corporate structure, or other similar equity restructuring transaction, our board of directors will make appropriate and proportionate adjustments to: (1) the class(es) and maximum number of shares reserved under our ESPP, (2) the class(es) and maximum number of shares by which the share reserve may increase automatically each year, (3) the class(es) and number of shares subject to, and purchase price applicable to, all outstanding offerings and purchase rights, and (4) the class(es) and number of shares that are subject to purchase limits under ongoing offerings.

Dissolution or Liquidation. In the event of a dissolution or liquidation of the company, any offering then in

progress will be shortened by setting a new purchase date prior to the consummation of such proposed dissolution or liquidation. The board of directors will notify each participant that the purchase date has been changed and that the participant’s purchase right will be exercised automatically on the new purchase date unless prior to such date the participant has withdrawn from the offering.

Corporate Transactions. Our ESPP provides that in the event of certain significant corporate transactions,

including: (1) a transfer of all or substantially all of our assets; (2) a merger, consolidation, or other capital, reorganization or business combination transaction of the company with or into another corporation, entity, or person; or (3) the consummation of a transaction, or series of related transactions, in which any person becomes the beneficial owner, directly or indirectly, of more than 50% of our then outstanding capital stock, a successor corporation may assume, continue, or substitute each outstanding purchase right. If the successor corporation does not assume, continue, or substitute the purchase right, the participants’ accumulated contributions will be used to purchase shares prior to the corporate transaction and the purchase rights will terminate immediately after such purchase. The administrator will notify each participant that the purchase date has been changed and that the participant’s purchase right will be exercised automatically on the new purchase date unless prior to such date the participant has withdrawn from the offering.

Spin-Off. In the event of a spin-off or similar transaction, our board of directors may take actions deemed

necessary or appropriate in connection with any ongoing offering, subject to compliance with applicable laws. In the absence of any such action, if an entity ceases to qualify as a related company under our ESPP, any participant in an ongoing offering employed by, or providing services to, such entity will be treated in the same manner as if the participant had terminated employment or service.

Amendment or Termination. Our board of directors has the authority to amend, suspend, or terminate our

ESPP at any time, except that, subject to certain exceptions described in our ESPP, no such action may materially impair outstanding rights under our ESPP. We will obtain stockholder approval of any amendment to our ESPP as required by applicable law or listing requirements.
