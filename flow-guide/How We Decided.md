# One decision, with its trail intact

**Decision.** Fund the referral motion with $18k in Q4 and pause paid self-serve acquisition. **Decided.** 7 October 2026. **Approved by.** Both founders. **Status.** Agreed, with one condition attached below.

This document exists because [[Customer Interviews]] found that every enterprise account asked for exactly this: a record of what changed and who agreed to it.

## How the question was framed

The framing came first, before any analysis:

> Six enterprise deals closed this quarter and five were referrals. Self-serve conversion halved. Should we move acquisition budget from self-serve to referrals for Q4?

Framing it as a budget question rather than "why did self-serve drop" is what kept it decidable in a week. The diagnostic question is still open, and is still worth answering — it just was not what needed deciding.

## What the evidence supported

| Claim | Source | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referrals produced 5 of 6 enterprise wins | CRM export | Strong — full population, not a sample |
| Referred deals close 17 days faster | [[Quarterly Business Review]] | Moderate — n=6 |
| Self-serve conversion halved | Product analytics | Strong |
| Competitor pricing caused the drop | [[Market Landscape]] | **Weak — correlation only** |
| Customers expand without being sold | [[Customer Interviews]] | Moderate — sample favours expansion |

The weak row is deliberately in the table. The decision was made *despite* not knowing whether price caused the self-serve drop, and hiding that would make this record useless the next time someone asks why.

## The disagreement

One founder argued for pausing self-serve entirely and reallocating the whole budget. The other argued that one quarter following a pricing change is not enough to abandon a channel. The condition below is the compromise that got both signatures:

> Pause paid acquisition, keep the self-serve product and its free tier untouched, and revisit in January with a proper attribution study. If conversion recovers on its own, the price hypothesis was wrong and we should know that before Q1 planning.

## Approval, and why it is a feature

Nothing above was written into this folder without someone reading it and saying yes. That is the part worth noticing: the gate is not friction bolted onto a generation step, it is the thing that makes this document usable as a record months later. An unapproved change would leave you unable to tell what a person decided from what a model proposed.

## Run one yourself

The cheapest way to understand the gate is to pass something through it.

1. Select the disagreement paragraph above.
2. Ask Flow to sharpen it — the condition is wordier than it needs to be.
3. Read what comes back **before** approving: the proposed text, what it would replace, which model produced it, and what the run cost.
4. Approve it, or decline it. Either way the document is yours; declining leaves the paragraph exactly as it is.

You can also ask Flow to evaluate one of these documents against a quality rubric and tell you where it is weak. Nothing in this folder ships with a stored score, deliberately — a score computed on someone else's machine, about someone else's draft, is not evidence about yours.
