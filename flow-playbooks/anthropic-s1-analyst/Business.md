# Business

## Overview

For the first time in history, intelligence is something humans can build. The cognitive capability to reason, analyze, synthesize, decide, and create is producible at scale and accessible on demand. We believe this abundance of AI will rapidly transform every sector of the global economy in ways greater than the impact of industrialization, electricity, semiconductors, and the internet. AI that truly delivers on its potential—with the risks well managed—could significantly accelerate global growth and dramatically improve quality of life for people around the world.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by a team of researchers responsible for many of the breakthroughs that define AI. We are first and foremost a research lab—our mission is the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. This mission directs our focus—along with our talent and culture, our frontier and safety research, our approach to compute, and our enterprise strategy, it is one of the core advantages that set us apart. These advantages enable us to continuously advance frontier AI and shape the standards for its safe and responsible development. Our frontier AI, Claude, is delivered to customers through our models, platform, and applications. Claude has experienced the fastest enterprise adoption of any technology in history, as demonstrated by revenue growing from $0 at commercial launch in the first quarter of 2023 to $ for the three months ended September 30, 2026. ### Claude is Accelerating Our Ability to Close the Loop

Claude itself has become one of the most powerful drivers of our progress—each new version of a Claude model directly accelerates development of the next. Many aspects of the research, training, and testing of subsequent model generations now involve Claude, including running experiments, generating and processing training data, automating evaluations and safety audits, and writing and reviewing code. We refer to this recursive dynamic as “closing the loop.” A direct expression of this dynamic is our accelerating pace of producing compute multipliers. More capable models help our researchers discover these improvements faster; in turn, more powerful compute multipliers enable us to train even more capable models and deploy them more efficiently.

A clear example of our ability to close the loop is the deployment of Claude Code, our agentic coding product, across our engineering organization. As our models have advanced, we have seen significant improvement in Claude’s capability on more challenging, open-ended engineering problems that lack a clear specification and have historically depended on experienced human judgment. Claude’s success rate on these problems reached approximately 90% in September 2026, up approximately 50 percentage points in nine months, based on our internal benchmarks. Claude Code has progressed from suggesting code to writing and running it directly and working autonomously over long time horizons on complex, multi-step engineering tasks. This has enabled our engineers to increasingly direct and review its work rather than write code line by line. As a result, the average daily volume of code contributed to our codebase per engineer in the three months ended September 30, 2026 was over 45 times the average daily volume in the year ended December 31, 2024. Over 90% of the contributions to our codebase in September 2026 were written by Claude.

The impact of Claude extends beyond research and engineering. From December 31, 2024 to September 30, 2026, our headcount grew over five times, while our revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was 86 times our revenue for the three months ended December 31, 2024.

## AI is Transforming Intelligence

We believe that Powerful AI, a highly capable and impactful form of frontier AI characterized by the following key attributes, will become widely available within a few years:

•cognitive capabilities matching or exceeding those of top human experts across most professional and

scientific disciplines;

•the ability to navigate all interfaces available to a human working virtually, including text, audio, video,

and direct computer control;

•the ability to reason through complex tasks autonomously over extended periods—lasting hours, days,

or weeks—and seek clarification and feedback when needed;

•the ability to coordinate across multiple instances and collaborate with both humans and other AI

•the ability to interface with the physical world by digitally controlling laboratory equipment, robotic

systems, and other machines through digital connections.

If Powerful AI is directed at solving humanity’s hardest problems and the risks are managed responsibly, the potential is extraordinary. The possibilities include compressing decades of scientific progress into years; accelerating drug discovery, disease detection, and the delivery of personalized therapy; providing individually tailored education and expert financial and legal advice; advancing power grids and battery design for greater efficiency and reliability; and extending to the developing world capabilities that took wealthy nations generations to build.

### Scaling Laws and the Predictability of Progress

The intelligence and capabilities of AI systems have advanced dramatically. Tasks that were beyond the reach of AI systems just a few years ago, such as complex mathematical reasoning, designing scientific experiments, and executing substantial portions of software development, are now routinely performed by AI.

These advances have followed empirical “Scaling Laws,” the finding that AI model performance improves

predictably with increases in model size, training data, and compute. The foundational paper, Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models, whose lead authors (including Dario Amodei, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and

Tom Brown) later co-founded Anthropic, was critical in establishing these relationships and helped guide the wave of modern LLMs.

We expect AI capabilities—across language, reasoning, mathematics, coding, scientific analysis, and an expanding range of agentic tasks and knowledge work—to continue advancing for many years. As a result, we believe this will drive demand for AI that outstrips supply for the foreseeable future, limited principally by the availability of compute. ### The Importance of Safety and Ethical Behavior

While the potential benefits of AI are extraordinary, its rapid advancement will impact the economy and society in ways that could create significant disruption, particularly if the technology is not developed and deployed responsibly. For example, bad actors have already conducted high-profile cyberattacks using AI’s capabilities to discover vulnerabilities, generate malware, and evade detection tools; other risks associated with misuse, including in the domain of biology, could result in even greater harm. AI deployed at scale also has the potential to concentrate power and wealth both within and between countries in ways that could erode trust, harm society, and destabilize the geopolitical order. Misaligned AI systems could corrupt institutional decision- making or cause large-scale harm through autonomous actions that are not visible or understandable to their users and operators. We believe that frontier AI companies must invest in safety and alignment to help ensure the responsible diffusion of this technology. We also believe that safety, alignment, and ethical behavior resonate particularly strongly with enterprises, where rigorous safeguards, predictable model behavior, and demonstrable accountability have become decisive criteria in selecting an AI partner for mission-critical workloads.

### Our Approach to Compute

Our approach to compute starts with training models that are designed to be more capable and run more efficiently on any given type of compute.

Our ability to build more capable models and serve them more efficiently reflects the compounding returns of an iterative cycle between our pre-training, post-training, and inference research, all of which is enabled by our fungible compute pool. Pre- and post-training research produces proprietary compute multipliers that make our models more capable. Research and inference engineering drive sustained reductions in the compute required to serve those models.

In addition to enabling more capable and efficient models, our multi-chip compute architecture across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs, and AMD GPUs allows us to orchestrate workloads to optimize for price and performance. We have years of experience effectively training and running our models across multiple chip families, which we believe is differentiated and difficult to replicate. This foundation, paired with Claude’s increasing understanding of chip and model architectures, allows us to onboard new chip platforms in a fraction of the time this once required.

Our compute pool is fungible by design. We have made substantial engineering investments to build the internal orchestration, scheduling, and capacity management systems that allow accelerator capacity, regardless of provider, chip architecture, or physical location, to be directed across workloads. The same infrastructure that serves customer workloads is also directed to research, model development, and internal usage of Claude. These systems manage the underlying hardware as a unified capacity pool, monitor utilization and demand across that pool, and direct compute to wherever it is expected to be most productive at any given time.

Our multi-chip compute approach provides us with substantially greater chip access than relying on one platform and enables us to opportunistically capture the price-performance benefits of each chip family and generation. Securing this access requires significant advance commitment. Given the long lead times between contracting for compute and that compute becoming available to serve workloads, we secure compute well in advance of anticipated demand. In some cases, we may also make payments in advance of the delivery of the equipment or infrastructure. We anticipate making significant investments for the foreseeable future, including in compute and infrastructure, to ensure we have the technological resources to maintain and expand our position at the frontier of AI.

### The Claude Platform

#### Platform Features

Platform features enable developers to customize their usage of Claude, including:

•Extended Thinking. Claude can perform additional internal reasoning before producing a final

response, working through complex, multi-step problems to improve response quality on tasks like analysis, math, and coding.

•Adaptive Thinking. Claude automatically calibrates whether a given request calls for extended

•Effort Controls. Developers can specify an effort level on each request to guide how much reasoning

Claude applies, allowing them to balance response quality against cost and latency.

•Tool Use. Claude can natively call external functions and APIs throughout a conversation, with support

for parallel execution and interleaved thinking between tool calls. The search tool loads only the tools required for a given task on demand, which can reduce token overhead compared to loading full tool libraries upfront.

•Long Context. Claude supports large context windows and maintains its strong performance even as

inputs grow, meaning that developers can include entire codebases, large document sets, or long agent histories in a single request.

•Structured Outputs. Developers can constrain Claude’s responses to a specified schema, such as valid

JSON (JavaScript Object Notation), so results integrate reliably with downstream systems and application code with less additional parsing or validation.

•Code Execution. A sandboxed execution environment where Claude can write, run, and iterate on code

within an agent loop, which is foundational for data analysis and automation workflows. This environment lets Claude call tools and process large volumes of data more efficiently and run the scripts packaged in Agent Skills.

•Web Search. A native tool giving Claude access to real-time web information, enabling grounded,

citation-aware responses in production applications.

•Files API. Allows developers to upload documents once and reference them across multiple sessions,

simplifying workflows that depend on persistent documents.

#### Harnesses

A model alone is not an agent. Running one reliably in production is challenging, because agents must survive crashes and interruptions, act on systems with scoped and auditable credentials, and scale across variable workloads. Autonomous operation requires a harness, which provides the secure environment, credentials, memory, and orchestration loop a model needs to plan, call tools, verify its work, and complete a task safely on its own. Building and operating that infrastructure is specialized, continuous work that, until recently, each customer had to take on themselves. We offer two ways to run a harness on the Claude Platform:

•Agent SDK. This solution makes the same production-grade harness that powers Claude Code available

to all developers, to run on their own compute. It provides built-in context management, session handling, multi-agent coordination, and prompt optimization, so customers can build and run sophisticated agents on their own infrastructure in a fraction of the time otherwise required.

•Claude Managed Agents. An out-of-the-box solution in which we operate the harness on our

customers’ behalf, on Anthropic-managed compute. We host the sandboxes, store the credentials, maintain the memory, and run the agent loop; customers define their agent’s tasks, tools, and guardrails. When a new model becomes available, customers do not need to rebuild their agent loop.

### Our Data Center Strategy

Historically, we purchased compute under fixed-time agreements. As our compute requirements have scaled, we have transitioned to a hybrid model in which, in addition to purchasing compute as a cloud service, we also procure specialized AI accelerators directly and operate this hardware in dedicated data center facilities leased and operated on our behalf, to our specification. We refer to this additional source of compute as Anthropic Direct. This transition is driven in part by the scale of our compute requirements as well as the potential long- term economic advantages of direct infrastructure leasing and hosting. Our first data center facility became operational this year.

Anthropic Direct also lets us separate our commitment to data center capacity from our commitment to the AI accelerators deployed inside it. Because data centers require longer lead times but represent a smaller fraction of total compute costs, this approach enables us to secure data center capacity early while deferring larger accelerator investments until our specific compute needs are more certain.

Our data center infrastructure strategy includes several contractual arrangements that, taken together, form a new source of compute to supplement the capacity we procure from our CSPs. Such arrangements include the following:

•Compute Infrastructure Equipment. We procure specialized AI accelerators and associated

## Research Lab Identity and the Research-to-Product Pipeline

We are first and foremost a research lab. We develop the foundational science to train frontier models, perform rigorous research to understand these models’ inner workings, and develop techniques to align these models to human values and make them safe.

### Research Philosophy

Our research culture is organized around a principle we apply across the company: do the simple thing that works. We pursue continuous, empirically validated improvements to data composition, model architecture, and training methodology, all of which must demonstrate measurable gains that hold as models increase in scale. This is why we currently focus on software development and agentic capabilities: these are areas where economic impact is large; where safety concerns manifest, making them a test bed for safety research; and where outputs are largely verifiable.

Every research project must ultimately advance our mission. This focus also guides what we choose not to pursue. For example, we do not currently focus on developing video and image generation models, despite their commercial appeal. That compute instead goes to coding, agentic skills, and safety research—a research decision that has also aided the capital efficiency of our models.

Our research culture is highly collaborative and transparent. When multiple teams pursue parallel approaches, the norm is to consolidate behind the approach with the best empirical results rather than the loudest voice or most senior person in the room. This requires institutional trust we have built since our earliest days, and we believe it is a primary reason our research velocity has scaled significantly faster than headcount.

### How Research Becomes Product

Anthropic was founded on the empirical conviction that Scaling Laws are the primary engine of AI progress. We operate with a sustained rigor in how we allocate research resources, including talent and compute, and measure whether each investment translates into scaled model performance. We believe that sustained and focused research can allow us to advance at an accelerated rate beyond what Scaling Laws alone predict through our compute multiplier advancements that have allowed us to push the frontier of model capability with better compute efficiency.

Pre-training. Our pre-training process operates as a disciplined pipeline. Researchers propose potential

improvements to model architecture, training methodology, or data composition, and each candidate is then validated at a smaller scale before we permit it to consume large amounts of compute. We set a high bar for inclusion in a frontier training run; we require evidence that demonstrates a given improvement holds as models increase in size. Frontier training runs are themselves planned capital deployments with defined inputs and strict inclusion criteria. Post-training. Pre-training produces raw model capability. During post-training, that capability is refined into

This phase has evolved from a minor adjustment step into one of the largest areas of our research investment, reflecting evolution in model capability from conversational assistants to autonomous agents that perform tasks independently. Post-training centers on training models against complete, realistic tasks whose complexity reflects the demands of real professional environments. We have made substantial proprietary investments in developing this task portfolio, and its breadth and realism constitute one of our most durable scientific advantages, which we believe is difficult to observe from outside the organization and correspondingly difficult to replicate.

Inference. Our model architecture and inference infrastructure are co-designed. AI researchers and inference

infrastructure engineers share planning cycles, reporting lines, and performance targets. Model architecture decisions are made with inference economics as a priority, which is why successive versions of Claude models can deliver both higher capability and greater efficiency than their predecessors.

## Safety Research is Core to Our Business

Any technology that is powerful enough to produce the enormous benefits that we believe AI will bring also poses risks. Responding to these risks requires a portfolio of safety research that spans how we train, evaluate, interpret, secure, and monitor our models.

### Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy

Our Responsible Scaling Policy describes how we approach the catastrophic risks posed by AI. The policy requires us to regularly publish detailed risk reports that reflect our perspectives on the overall level of risk posed by our models and how we are mitigating such risks. Our teams test each model extensively before release, assessing its capabilities in the domains most relevant to catastrophic risk and evaluating the extent to which the model can be misused. Our risk report describes an overall assessment of the results of these evaluations, the mitigations we have implemented to address these risks, and whether, and if so, why, we believe the identified risks are justified by corresponding benefits. As part of the current version of our Responsible Scaling Policy, we publish a Frontier Safety Roadmap, detailing our plans for measurable advances

231

in security, alignment, safeguards, and AI policy. This acts as an internal spur for safety research and development and is externally visible so customers and regulators can use it to gauge our progress. Consistent with this approach, we assess the risks of each model during development and before and after release and we take the steps we believe are appropriate to mitigate those risks. In the past, these steps have included delaying the release of new models and limiting their initial availability, such as our initial release of Mythos through Project Glasswing, and in the future, as we learn more about model capabilities and behavior, could include additional measures that affect how and when we develop or release these new models. We believe these practices also serve our customers, since rigorous safeguards and predictable model behavior have become decisive criteria when selecting an AI partner.

### Claude’s Constitution

We have developed a constitution for Claude that describes our vision for how an AI model should be and how it should behave, which is used directly in the model training process. We have used Claude’s constitution to meaningfully advance how frontier models are trained, and it is our attempt to grapple with the most difficult questions regarding the development of this new type of artificially intelligent entity. It is a living document that we intend to revise as we develop further models and learn more about how they work. Our researchers have found that training Claude on high-quality constitutional documents combined with fictional stories portraying an aligned AI contributes to reducing Claude’s misalignment rate. We believe Claude’s constitution has also proven commercially valuable: transparently laying out the values we intend Claude to hold increases customer trust.

This constitution orients Claude around four priorities:

•Be Broadly Safe. Avoid irreversible actions and support human oversight of AI behavior.

•Be Broadly Ethical. Maintain honesty and sound moral judgment, and avoid harmful acts.

•Be Compliant with our Guidelines. Adhere to our operational standards for user interactions and

absolute prohibitions on certain categories of harm.

•Be Genuinely Helpful. Deliver substantive, expert-level assistance that creates real value for users.

### Model Validation and Training Data

Beyond the assessments performed under our Responsible Scaling Policy, we evaluate each Claude model prior to its release through a structured, multi-stage evaluation program covering model capabilities, safety behaviors, and societal impacts, including bias and discrimination, and we document the results of these pre- deployment evaluations, and their limitations, in our publicly available model system cards. This testing addresses the reliability and accuracy of model outputs, including through capability and accuracy benchmarking, evaluations focused on factual accuracy and hallucinations, evaluations of whether model outputs are honest and accurately reflect the information on which they are based, reliability testing of agentic performance over long-running tasks, and adversarial red-teaming designed to identify ways in which a model may fail or behave in unintended ways. Where these evaluations identify issues, we address them through additional post-training, adjusted safeguards, and re-evaluation prior to release, and we document residual limitations in the applicable model system card.

Claude models are trained on data drawn from a broad range of sources, modalities, and languages, including publicly available information from the internet, datasets licensed or commissioned from third parties, and other sources, such as synthetic data generated by our own models. We conduct diligence on third-party data sources to assess their quality, provenance, and suitability for our usage, and in many cases include contractual restrictions intended to discourage the inclusion of personal data in sourced datasets. We apply data curation and filtering processes to sourced data, which are designed to improve data quality, deduplicate content, and reduce potential exposure to harmful content.

We address bias and fairness risks at multiple stages of model training and deployment. We manage the representativeness and diversity of our training data through a layered approach in which dataset curation, post-training alignment, pre-deployment evaluation, and deployment safeguards each play a part, rather than through dataset composition alone. When deployed, our usage policy prohibits the use of Claude for

discriminatory decision-making and requires human oversight for high-stakes automated decisions, such as those affecting employment, housing, or legal issues. Our validation, safeguards, and monitoring practices continue to evolve as model capabilities advance and as new risks and failure modes are identified.

Further, we have obtained ISO/IEC 42001 certification, an international standard established by the International Organization for Standardization (“ISO”) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (“IEC”), following an independent third-party audit of our AI management system, covering risk identification, assessment, and mitigation processes for the responsible development and deployment of AI. ### Alignment

As AI models grow more capable, ensuring safe and predictable behavior becomes more challenging. Our Alignment team focuses on whether models behave as intended, and do not take unwanted actions of their own accord. The team evaluates models to surface undesirable behaviors and develops comprehensive safety evaluations, many of which we open source. Misaligned behaviors, whether occurring in testing or real-world deployments, have been observed across the industry and include examples of models circumventing network and sandbox restrictions, granting themselves permissions they had not been given, taking unintended actions in pursuit of assigned goals, interfering with other AI agents, and misrepresenting whether tasks had been completed or verified. As models are granted greater autonomy, permissions, and integration with internal and external systems, the consequences of such misaligned behavior may increase. For more information about the risks related to model misalignment, see the section titled “Risk Factors—Our AI models may behave in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways, including by taking harmful actions that no user or third party directed, which could adversely affect our reputation and brand, business, financial condition, and results of operations.” Our own internal alignment evaluation shows that our newly released frontier models have demonstrated improved alignment compared to their predecessors.

### Interpretability

Unlike traditional software, where unwanted outputs are traceable to specific bugs, the inner workings of LLMs like Claude are more inscrutable. Our researchers describe AI models as “grown, not built”—software developers can set the initial conditions and some constraints, but they cannot fully control any given output or behavior. This makes assurances about safety—and the diagnosis of problems—far more challenging for AI than for standard software. Anthropic is widely recognized as a leader in mechanistic interpretability: the science of how LLMs work. We have developed mathematical techniques to map how models form and connect concepts as they reason through problems; investigated how they reflect constructs analogous to psychological states and thought processes; and tested how their behavior can be steered through direct adjustments to internal representations. We use interpretability research along with other alignment research techniques to test for unwanted behavioral tendencies before a model release.

### Safeguards

Our Safeguards team reduces the risk of Claude producing dangerous, harmful, or undesirable content through multiple approaches, including probes and classifiers that monitor inputs and outputs, training against specific attack vectors such as prompt injection, and monitoring for user safety concerns including encouragement of harmful ideation. When we detect potential violations, enforcement actions range from real-time response adjustments to account warnings and, in severe cases, termination of the offending user’s accounts. Findings from ongoing monitoring, external expert stress-testing, and threat intelligence inform our policies, model training, and detection systems. Identified issues may be addressed through measures such as updates to detection classifiers, reward-model adjustments during training, and changes to the instructions provided to our models that guide their behavior (known as system prompts). We also maintain public reporting channels, including a responsible disclosure policy and a bug bounty program for testing our safety defenses.

233

We have observed that more capable models are generally better at recognizing and preventing high-stakes misuse. We believe that robust safety measures and model utility are not inherently in tension, and that safety can improve alongside the capability of the models themselves.

### Our Approach to Model Releases

Each release is accompanied by a detailed system card covering capability and safety evaluations, external tester reports, and discussion of areas where performance remains limited or where the model shows signs of unwanted behavior. For example, AI poses clear and present risks in the cybersecurity domain as advanced models could assist threat actors in security breaches and exploits. Mitigating these risks is a priority across our research, evaluation, and deployment practices. Before each model release, our Frontier Red Team conducts a detailed analysis to determine whether new models provide meaningful new capabilities to assist potential attackers, and our Safeguards team maintains dedicated classifiers and monitoring systems targeting cyber- related misuse, including prompts related to malware creation, phishing, and infrastructure attacks.

We make decisions about model development and deployment, including whether to begin training a new model, on a case-by-case basis. Before releasing a frontier model, we consider a variety of factors, such as the capabilities and behaviors of the specific model at issue and of our models overall, how well our safeguards and security measures work, and how widely the model would be available. We plan to extend our testing to include more assessments at each stage of model training: pre-training, post-training, and internal deployment. Depending on these assessments, we may delay or suspend training or related research, delay releases, or limit access to models until we have confidence they can be developed and deployed safely. Our assessments are also informed by voluntary pre-deployment evaluation of certain models by the CAISSI and the U.K. AI Security Institute. For certain models that materially advance the capability of the frontier in areas relevant to national security, we also provide designated government partners with expanded early access so that they can independently evaluate model capabilities and safeguards before release.

Based on our analyses, we do not release every model we develop for general use; when the model’s capabilities warrant caution, we may restrict availability or decline to release entirely. For example, in April 2026, we initially released our frontier model, Mythos, as a gated research preview through Project Glasswing, available to select organizations for cybersecurity applications, rather than making it broadly available at launch due to its advanced capabilities in cyber vulnerability discovery. We later released Fable 5 for general use, but with safety restrictions on certain cybersecurity, biology, and chemistry capabilities.

### Race to the Top

Building reliable, trustworthy, and secure AI systems cannot be the task of one AI company alone. Our goal is to foster a race to the top in which AI developers compete on the safety and security of their systems, rather than rushing to release increasingly powerful models without adequate safeguards. In addition to publishing a large volume of research, we open source our evaluations where possible and participate in groups such as the Frontier Model Forum to share safety-critical findings with other AI developers. We have also sought to lead by example in ways that shape industry norms. After we published the first version of our Responsible Scaling Policy in September 2023, two other leading frontier AI labs adopted similar frameworks within months.

Building on this approach, we have publicly called for “pacing the frontier,” by which we mean establishing industry-wide standards primarily related to the use of AI to accelerate AI development, so that AI capabilities do not advance faster than the safety, alignment, and security practices needed to support them. This call to pace the frontier contemplates frontier AI developers collaborating with governments and does not describe our own model development or release timelines, which continue to be informed by our risk assessments.
