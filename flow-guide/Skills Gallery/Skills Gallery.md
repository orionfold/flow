# Skills Gallery

**Choose the skill by the question your text is asking.** A skill is a method Flow applies to your words: it reads a passage and draws the answer as a chart, a diagram, a table, a form or a figure. What it draws waits in **Review Changes** until you keep it.

Start with [[Skills — Living Example]]: a map an Opportunity Map run drew, kept current from a table you can edit. Then pick a question below. Every page shows a short passage and what the skill drew from it. The passages and numbers are fictional teaching examples.

## Run one

1. Select the passage the skill should read. With nothing selected, it reads the whole document.
2. Open **Elements** and press the skill's card under **Skills**.
3. Read what it drew in **Review Changes**, then keep or discard it.

A skill uses only what your text says. When the text lacks something the method needs, such as a date or an owner, the skill says so in a line instead of guessing. **Settings ▸ Skills** lists every skill, and its switch hides a card you never use.

## Make it yours

1. Copy the entire **Skills Gallery** folder in Finder, then add the copy to Flow.
2. Open [[Skills Input]]. Under **Initiatives**, use **View ▸ Edit Table** to change Dark mode's value from **3 to 7** and its quadrant to **Quick win**, then save.
3. Open [[Skills — Living Example]] and choose **Settings ▸ Jobs ▸ Run all Jobs**. Expect Dark mode's dot to rise into the quick-win corner, and the summary to show **2 quick wins**.
4. Replace the four fictional initiatives with your own, scored 1 to 10 for value and effort. Keep the Initiatives heading and its columns.

## Read the drawing honestly

| Before relying on what a skill drew | Check |
| --- | --- |
| A score or a placement | Whose judgement it records, and that the text supports it |
| A date or a duration | That the text states it, not that the skill assumed it |
| A missing-information line | What to add to the text before running it again |
| A figure you can press | That every note it shows is in the text |

A skill draws once, from the passage it read. Editing the drawing, or keeping it current from a table, does not make the claim behind it true.
