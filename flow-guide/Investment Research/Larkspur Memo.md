# Larkspur Memo

**Fictional investment research exercise · no recommendation to invest**

## The business may be promising; the supplied evidence is incomplete

Larkspur is an invented maker of inspection systems for food-processing plants. The exercise proposes a $12M financing round and a $1.5M check for discussion. No valuation conclusion is supported by the materials in this folder.

| Management representation | Earlier sample | Latest sample | Verification needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ARR | $410k | $1.6M | Recurring software versus hardware/service revenue |
| Plants live | 6 | 19 | Customer list and operating status |
| Gross margin | 41% | 54% | Cost allocation and installation economics |
| CEO-led sales | Not supplied | 16 of 19 wins | Deal ownership and repeatability |

## Questions before an investment view

1. Separate recurring revenue from hardware and one-off installation work. “ARR” is not meaningful until that policy is clear.
2. Reconcile plant counts, contracts and collections. A signed contract is different from a live installation.
3. Obtain reliability and service records. An image is not evidence of field performance.
4. Test sales transferability using actual opportunities owned by the new team.
5. Build a cash forecast before using “default alive” or a runway number.

The next step is evidence collection, recorded in [[Diligence Brief]]. Any real investment decision needs a fuller diligence process and the decision-maker’s own criteria.
