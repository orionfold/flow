## What this Domain is

This Domain is a reader's guide to the Form S-1 registration statement that Anthropic, PBC filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. The filing is split into the parts listed below, and at roughly 239,000 words it is too long for most people to read cover to cover. The guide suggests an order to read it in and five questions to keep in mind (listed in the front matter), so that a retail investor can understand what the company does, how it makes and spends money, what could go wrong, and what buying a share actually gives them.

## Reading order

1. **Start with the overview.** [Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md), then [Special Note Regarding Forward-looking Statements](Special%20Note%20Regarding%20Forward-looking%20Statements.md), so you know which statements are forecasts rather than facts. [Table of Contents to Select Defined Terms](Table%20of%20Contents%20to%20Select%20Defined%20Terms.md) explains the filing's own vocabulary, such as its Applied AI teams of forward-deployed engineers.
2. **See what is being sold.** [The Offering](The%20Offering.md) and [Summary Consolidated Financial and Other Data](Summary%20Consolidated%20Financial%20and%20Other%20Data.md) for the shares on offer and the headline figures.
3. **Understand the business.** [Business](Business.md) covers who uses Claude and how: knowledge workers on Claude Pro and Claude Max, enterprises served by a hybrid sales and engineering function, and acquisitions and partnerships such as Coefficient Bio and Ode with Anthropic. Then read [Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations](Management%E2%80%99s%20Discussion%20and%20Analysis%20of%20Financial%20Condition%20and%20Results%20of%20Operations.md), where management explains why the numbers moved (question 1).
4. **Weigh the business risks.** [Risks Related to our Business and Industry](Risks%20Related%20to%20our%20Business%20and%20Industry.md), followed by the infrastructure risks in [Supply and Concentration of Chips, Memory, and Data Center Equipment](Supply%20and%20Concentration%20of%20Chips,%20Memory,%20and%20Data%20Center%20Equipment.md), [Electrical Power and Fuel Constraints](Electrical%20Power%20and%20Fuel%20Constraints.md) and [Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md) (question 2). The rest of the business and industry risks are in [Key Personnel, Capital Needs, and Culture](Key%20Personnel,%20Capital%20Needs,%20and%20Culture.md), [Competition, Customers, and Market Adoption](Competition,%20Customers,%20and%20Market%20Adoption.md) and [AI Safety, Misuse, Liability, and Political Scrutiny](AI%20Safety,%20Misuse,%20Liability,%20and%20Political%20Scrutiny.md). The other risk parts, on [privacy and IP](Risks%20Related%20to%20Privacy,%20Cybersecurity,%20and%20Intellectual%20Property.md), [legal and regulatory matters](Risks%20Related%20to%20Legal%20and%20Regulatory%20Matters.md) and [tax and accounting](Risks%20Related%20to%20Tax%20and%20Accounting%20Matters.md), can be read after that.
5. **Read the governance.** [Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md), then [Risks Related to our Governance Structure](Risks%20Related%20to%20our%20Governance%20Structure.md) and [Internal Governance and Decision-Making](Internal%20Governance%20and%20Decision-Making.md), with [Management](Management.md) and [Executive Compensation](Executive%20Compensation.md) for who runs the company and how they are paid (question 3).
6. **Look at your stake.** [Use of Proceeds](Use%20of%20Proceeds.md), [Dilution](Dilution.md), [Dividend Policy to Capitalization](Dividend%20Policy%20to%20Capitalization.md), [Principal and Selling Stockholders](Principal%20and%20Selling%20Stockholders.md), [Description of Capital Stock](Description%20of%20Capital%20Stock.md), [Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md) and [Risks Related to Ownership of our Class A Common Stock](Risks%20Related%20to%20Ownership%20of%20our%20Class%20A%20Common%20Stock.md) (questions 4 and 5). [Certain Relationships and Related Person Transactions](Certain%20Relationships%20and%20Related%20Person%20Transactions.md) shows dealings with insiders.
7. **Go to the source figures.** The auditor's reports, in [Report Of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm](Report%20Of%20Independent%20Registered%20Public%20Accounting%20Firm.md) and [the second report](Report%20Of%20Independent%20Registered%20Public%20Accounting%20Firm-2.md), for readers who want to check the numbers themselves. [Underwriting](Underwriting.md), [Material U.S. Federal Income Tax Consequences to Non-U.S. Holders of Our Class A Common Stock](Material%20U.S.%20Federal%20Income%20Tax%20Consequences%20to%20Non-U.S.%20Holders%20of%20Our%20Class%20A%20Common%20Stock.md), [Part II](Part%20II.md) and the [Exhibit Index](Exhibit%20Index.md) are reference material.

## The five questions, and where the filing answers them

This version of the filing leaves many prices, share counts and dollar amounts blank until the offering is priced. Where a figure is missing below, the filing has not given it yet. The section “What this S-1 leaves blank”, further down, lists the gaps.

### 1. How does Anthropic make money, and is revenue growing faster than the cost of the compute behind it?

- Claude reaches customers through the Claude models, Claude Platform, and applications such as Claude Code and Claude Cowork. These are sold directly and through AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. Anthropic contracts with the customer itself and pays each cloud provider a fee ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).
- Revenue was $41 million in 2023, $386 million in 2024 and $4,585 million in 2025. In the first half of 2026 it was $16,286 million, against $1,163 million a year earlier ([Summary Consolidated Financial and Other Data](Summary%20Consolidated%20Financial%20and%20Other%20Data.md)).
- Compute and infrastructure cost $433 million, $2,522 million and $7,326 million in those three years, and $8,945 million in the first half of 2026. That half-year is the first period in the table where revenue is larger than compute cost ([Summary Consolidated Financial and Other Data](Summary%20Consolidated%20Financial%20and%20Other%20Data.md)).
- The first half of 2026 still shows an operating loss of $693 million. The net loss of $80,748 million is mostly a $72,388 million change in the fair value of convertible notes and similar instruments, which the filing ties to the company's rising valuation. On the company's own non-GAAP measure, Adjusted Operating Income was $1,618 million, a 10% margin ([Summary Consolidated Financial and Other Data](Summary%20Consolidated%20Financial%20and%20Other%20Data.md)).
- The company says revenue grew 1,368% year over year in the quarter to June 30, 2026, while compute and infrastructure expenses grew 300%. It reports Net Revenue Retention of about 900%. These are management's own figures, and the risk summary warns that past growth rates do not indicate future performance ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).
- Compute and infrastructure commitments run as far as 2045. Their total is left blank in this version ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).

### 2. How exposed is the business to a few suppliers of chips, memory, electrical power and data centres?

- Most of the world's advanced chip manufacturing is in Taiwan, mainly at TSMC. The company says it has not always been able to get enough chips on time ([Supply and Concentration of Chips, Memory, and Data Center Equipment](Supply%20and%20Concentration%20of%20Chips,%20Memory,%20and%20Data%20Center%20Equipment.md)).
- High bandwidth memory comes largely from three makers: SK hynix, Samsung and Micron ([Supply and Concentration of Chips, Memory, and Data Center Equipment](Supply%20and%20Concentration%20of%20Chips,%20Memory,%20and%20Data%20Center%20Equipment.md)).
- Transformers, switchgear, turbines and generators can take years to arrive, and the company may lose pre-delivery payments if its plans change. U.S. export controls could limit where data centres can be built ([Supply and Concentration of Chips, Memory, and Data Center Equipment](Supply%20and%20Concentration%20of%20Chips,%20Memory,%20and%20Data%20Center%20Equipment.md)).
- Grid power is a constraint, with lead times of years. Some planned sites rely on on-site natural gas generation, and the company bears fuel costs on a pass-through basis without cost caps. It has also publicly committed to cover electricity price increases to consumers caused by its data centres ([Electrical Power and Fuel Constraints](Electrical%20Power%20and%20Fuel%20Constraints.md)).
- Compute is spread across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs and AMD GPUs. The risk summary still flags reliance on a limited number of cloud providers and suppliers, some of which are also investors and customers ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).
- Building a data centre takes long permitting, specialised equipment and scarce skilled trades such as electricians, and shortages can stretch timelines and raise costs. A limited number of outside firms build, manage and run some of the company's facilities. Some of them have little operating history, and some are trained by chip partners without being their affiliates ([Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md)).
- Some capacity depends on financing arrangements, or on partners who guarantee the lease obligations for sites that will host that partner's chips ([Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md)).
- The infrastructure is concentrated mainly in the United States. Some states and localities are considering or have approved moratoriums on new data centres. New York has paused state permitting for new large facilities, and Texas can curtail or disconnect large electricity loads in grid emergencies. Opposition is becoming more organised, and the loss of a major facility may not be readily absorbed by the rest ([Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md)).
- To reduce that concentration, the company has signed letters of intent and is negotiating leases for sites in several countries outside the U.S. That depends on forming local subsidiaries, getting government approvals and setting up local banking. It also brings foreign regulatory, data-localisation and tax risks ([Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md)).
- Competitors that control more of the supply chain, through their own chips, data centres or dedicated power, may get compute at greater scale, lower cost or with more reliability ([Data Center Infrastructure](Data%20Center%20Infrastructure.md)).
- So far, compute has been paid for mainly through cloud providers, with costs recognised as capacity arrives. Paying more of it up front would mean cash going out before the compute or the revenue arrives. Some draws under the note purchase agreements depend on meeting revenue targets and on third parties delivering compute ([Key Personnel, Capital Needs, and Culture](Key%20Personnel,%20Capital%20Needs,%20and%20Culture.md)).

### 3. What does the public benefit corporation structure mean for the rights and returns of Class A shareholders?

- Directors must balance three things: stockholders' financial interests, the interests of those affected by the company's conduct, and the Company Purpose. The company says this may lead to decisions that hurt the share price. There is also little court precedent on how such balancing is judged ([Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md)).
- Public benefit performance is not measured against any objective third-party standard. The board sets its own standards, expects them to be qualitative, and reports to stockholders at least every two years ([Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md)).
- The Long-Term Benefit Trust elects the Class T directors, who make up a majority of the board, but it has no say in day-to-day management. It has five trustee seats, one of them vacant. Each trustee is paid $400,000 a year and may not hold the company's shares ([Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md)).
- A Class A share carries one vote and a pro rata share of dividends and liquidation proceeds. Class T, F and E shares carry no dividend rights. As a Nasdaq controlled company, Anthropic plans to use the exemption only for its nominating and corporate governance committee ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).
- The company gave 846,840,960 Class N shares, 5% of fully diluted shares, to its own Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. The board may issue up to a further 2% each time the share price rises 50% above the previous milestone ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).

### 4. How much dilution do new buyers take, and who keeps voting control after the offering?

- Dilution is the gap between the offering price and net tangible book value per share. Both, and the resulting dilution per share, are left blank in this version ([Dilution](Dilution.md)).
- More dilution can come later from options, RSUs, shares issued for acquisitions after September 30, 2026, and the 2026 Plan and ESPP, which grow automatically each year ([Dilution](Dilution.md), [The Offering](The%20Offering.md)).
- A single Class F share, held by a company owned by the seven co-founders, carries 50.1% of total voting power. Future share issues do not reduce that share, so the founders can decide stockholder votes, including on a merger or sale ([Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md)).
- Together, the trust and the founders' company elect every director. Founder control ends after a 450-day transition that starts once there have been fewer than three Continuing Founders for 90 days in a row ([Our Governance Structure and Its Goals](Our%20Governance%20Structure%20and%20Its%20Goals.md)).
- Class N shares carry no vote and convert to Class A when sold to an unaffiliated buyer ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).

### 5. Where does the money raised go, who is selling, and when can existing holders sell more?

- The company plans to use the proceeds for general corporate purposes, including compute, infrastructure and hiring. Part will pay the tax withholding on the RSU Settlement. It has no agreements for any material acquisitions and keeps broad discretion over how the money is spent ([Use of Proceeds](Use%20of%20Proceeds.md)).
- The company receives nothing from shares sold by selling stockholders, and the number of shares they will sell is still blank ([The Offering](The%20Offering.md)).
- As of June 30, 2026, the company had raised $141.1 billion in total and held $112.4 billion in cash and cash equivalents plus $19.3 billion in short-term investments ([Prospectus Summary](Prospectus%20Summary.md)).
- Five co-founders are listed as selling stockholders: Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah. Each beneficially owns between about 232 million and 244 million Class A shares. The number each will sell is blank, as it is for Daniela Amodei, who holds about 241 million. The table shows a dash for shares offered by Dario Amodei, who holds about 245 million including 4,621,120 shares he votes under proxies, and for the other directors and officers ([Principal and Selling Stockholders](Principal%20and%20Selling%20Stockholders.md)).
- Directors, officers and substantially all stockholders are under lock-ups, but these lift in stages rather than all at once. Some shares held by non-founder directors, officers, employees and consultants can be sold from the first trading day after the prospectus, with further releases on set days after that and after the 2026 annual results. The 180-day lock-up ends by the earlier of the second full trading day after the Q1 2027 results or day 180. The seven co-founders and certain other holders are locked up until the earlier of the second full trading day after the Q3 2027 results or day 365, with staged releases before then. The share counts for each date are blank ([Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md)).
- Class N shares are not listed, but they become Class A shares on a qualifying transfer. More Class N shares will be issued after the offering, from convertible notes that could not convert at once because of ownership caps, from future note draws, from the Donation Matching Plan and from private placements ([Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md)).
- Once lock-ups end, affiliates may sell in any three months no more than the greater of 1% of the shares outstanding or the average weekly trading volume over the four weeks before. Holders of Rule 701 shares must wait 90 days after the prospectus. Holders with registration rights under the investors' rights agreement and the Google registration rights agreement can ask for their shares to be registered, which could bring large blocks to market ([Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md)).
- The company plans to file Form S-8 soon after the offering. That makes shares under the 2021 Plan, 2026 Plan and ESPP eligible for sale, subject to vesting and lock-ups ([Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md)).

## What this S-1 leaves blank

This is the first, preliminary version of the S-1. Many figures are blank in the filing as submitted to the SEC: the gaps are in the original, not in this copy. Where a figure is missing, the filing still prints the dollar or percent sign and the words around it, so you will see phrases such as “between $ and $ ” with nothing in between.

**What is blank**

- **The price and size of the offering.** The price range, the number of shares sold by the company and by the selling stockholders, the underwriters’ option, the underwriting discount, the proceeds, the delivery date and the date of the prospectus itself ([Anthropic, PBC](Anthropic,%20PBC.md)).
- **Shares outstanding and net proceeds.** The share counts after the offering for Class A, Class N and all classes together, the estimated net proceeds, the assumed offering price, the principal amount of convertible notes converting into Class N shares, the assumed tax withholding rate for the RSU Settlement, and the numbers of options, RSUs and plan shares excluded from the count. The single Class T and Class F shares, and zero Class E shares, are given ([The Offering](The%20Offering.md)).
- **Dilution.** Historical, pro forma and pro forma as adjusted net tangible book value, the dilution per share to new investors, the sensitivity to each $1.00 change in price, and the table comparing what existing stockholders and new investors paid and will own ([Dilution](Dilution.md)).
- **Capitalization.** Every dollar figure in the capitalization table as of September 30, 2026, actual as well as pro forma, and the number of Class A and Class N shares issued. The authorized share counts are given ([Dividend Policy to Capitalization](Dividend%20Policy%20to%20Capitalization.md)).
- **Ownership and voting percentages.** Share counts for the named executives, directors and co-founders are given, but their percentages, voting power before and after the offering, and the shares each selling stockholder will sell are blank. The Founder LLC’s 50.1% of voting power is given ([Principal and Selling Stockholders](Principal%20and%20Selling%20Stockholders.md)).
- **Compute commitments.** The total committed spend on purchase commitments and leases, the part expected before December 31, 2030, and the part due in the next 12 months ([Business](Business.md)).
- **Lock-up releases.** The number of shares freed on each release date, and the percentages of each lock-up group they represent. Also the share of fully diluted shares under the 180-day and 365-day lock-ups, the number of shares with registration rights, and the 1% Rule 144 share count ([Shares Eligible for Future Sale](Shares%20Eligible%20for%20Future%20Sale.md)).

**The reason the filing gives**

The filing explains its blanks only in general terms. The cover says: “The information in this preliminary prospectus is not complete and may be changed. These securities may not be sold until the registration statement filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission is effective,” and is headed “Subject To Completion. Dated .” ([Anthropic, PBC](Anthropic,%20PBC.md)). Most of the blanks depend on the offering price, and the filing says those figures are “illustrative only and may change based on the actual initial public offering price and other terms of this offering” ([Dilution](Dilution.md)) and “will be adjusted based on the actual initial public offering price and other terms of this offering determined at pricing” ([Dividend Policy to Capitalization](Dividend%20Policy%20to%20Capitalization.md)).

The filing gives no specific reason for the blanks that do not depend on the price: the actual September 30, 2026 figures in the capitalization table, the historical net tangible book value, and the total of compute commitments. Expect them, with the price-based figures, in a later amended filing, and check the final prospectus before relying on any number here.
