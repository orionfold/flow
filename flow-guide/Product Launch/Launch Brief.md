# Launch Brief

**Cedar Notes website relaunch · fictional product brief · 25 September 2026**

## Help each buyer find the right product

The site has grown from one product to three. Its navigation still reflects the old catalogue, and every campaign needs engineering help to publish. The launch should improve product discovery and give the marketing team a clear publishing path.

## Two journeys shape the scope

| Visitor | Job to finish | Page must answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual evaluator | Decide which product to try | What it does, the relevant limits and a clear next step |
| Team buyer | Determine whether to investigate further | Fit for the work, deployment options and evidence to review |

These are authored hypotheses for this exercise. Validate them with observed sessions and support questions before treating them as research findings.

## What the launch includes

- A product navigation structure with explicit audience labels.
- Reusable product, evidence and campaign templates.
- A publishing workflow the marketing owner can operate.
- Redirects and analytics checks that preserve a way to compare before and after.

The proposal does not include a new product, a pricing change or a redesign of the application. [[Launch Plan]] owns measures, timing and responsibilities; [[Launch Milestones]] supplies the living readiness view.

## Decide the publishing boundary first

The CMS decision is about what marketing can change without engineering. More template freedom also brings more review and maintenance. The team needs a recorded choice before committing the build.

| Question | Evidence needed | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content only, or editable templates? | One real campaign published through each candidate | Dan + Mei |
| Which archive pages must move? | Traffic, link and support-use inventory | Dan |
| How will a release be reversed? | Rehearsed redirect and deployment rollback | Engineering owner |

The later [[Staff Meeting]] records the CMS choice. A recorded decision clears one dependency; it does not prove the build has finished.
