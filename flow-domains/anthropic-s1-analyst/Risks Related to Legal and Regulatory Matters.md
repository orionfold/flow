## Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters

We are subject to claims, lawsuits, investigations, executive and administrative orders and directives, other regulatory actions, and various actions and proceedings, and face potential liability, expenses for legal claims, and harm to our business.

We are subject to claims, lawsuits, arbitration proceedings, government inquiries and investigations, executive and administrative orders and directives, and other legal, regulatory, and administrative actions and proceedings, including those involving intellectual property disputes, compliance with regulatory requirements, government approvals and authorizations, labor and employment, anti-discrimination, commercial disputes, competition, privacy, consumer protection, customer complaints, advertising, and other matters relating to our technology, models, platform, and applications, and we may become subject to additional types of claims, lawsuits, arbitration proceedings, government inquiries and investigations, executive and administrative orders and directives, and other legal, regulatory, and administrative actions and proceedings as our business grows and as we deploy new technologies, models, platforms, and applications. In particular, as our models, platform, and applications increasingly include agentic AI systems capable of taking autonomous actions with real-world consequences, we may face novel legal theories of liability, including intellectual property infringement, product liability, agency, and negligence claims, that have not been tested in the context of autonomous AI systems and for which the scope of our potential exposure is uncertain. In addition, as we expand our healthcare and life sciences offerings, regulators may conclude that certain of our products, or our customers’ use of them, are subject to healthcare regulation or to other laws governing healthcare and health information, which could require us to obtain authorizations, modify or restrict our offerings, or incur significant compliance costs. Errors or other undesirable outputs from our models in these contexts could contribute to patient harm, compromised research, or regulatory failures by our customers, and could expose us to claims from customers, patients, and other third parties, as well as to regulatory inquiries and enforcement actions.

We are also subject to antitrust and competition law risk arising from our public advocacy regarding AI safety and the responsible pace of AI development and from our participation in industry groups, safety collaborations, and standard-setting initiatives. Communications, advocacy, or collaboration among companies that develop frontier AI models, or conduct alleged to reflect coordination among such companies, may be characterized as concerted action subject to the antitrust and competition laws, including Section 1 of the Sherman Act and analogous state and non-U.S. laws, even where undertaken for safety purposes, conducted publicly, or intended as advocacy for governmental action or industry standards. In September 2026, a putative class action was filed against us and other developers of frontier AI models in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging that we and the other defendants agreed to restrain competition by slowing the pace at which we develop and improve our respective AI models, in purported violation of Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The complaint seeks treble damages, injunctive relief, and other relief on behalf of a purported class of purchasers of the defendants’ paid consumer subscriptions. We disagree with the claims and intend to defend the matter vigorously, but the outcome of this and any similar proceeding is inherently uncertain.

Antitrust litigation can be complex, protracted, disruptive, and expensive, and an adverse outcome could result in treble damages and injunctive relief. We could also become subject to additional private litigation or to investigations or enforcement actions by the U.S. Department of Justice, the FTC, state attorneys general, or non-U.S. competition authorities. We have proposed public efforts to work with the U.S. government and other developers of frontier AI models to establish safety standards, including with respect to the pace of capability development, and have suggested that such collaboration may warrant a narrow exemption or safe harbor from antitrust laws. These proposals have drawn public opposition from political leaders, some of whom have characterized such efforts as anticompetitive regardless of the purpose of such exemption or safe harbor. There is no assurance that any such exemption or safe harbor will be adopted. Our advocacy for such an exemption, and any safety commitments we pursue with other developers of frontier AI in its absence, could subject us to additional regulatory, litigation, and other risks. These risks may cause us to limit or modify our public advocacy, our participation in industry safety and standard-setting initiatives, and our communications with other developers of frontier AI, which could adversely affect our ability to pursue collaborative approaches to AI safety that we believe are important to our mission.

The results of any such claims, lawsuits, arbitration proceedings, government inquiries and investigations, executive and administrative orders and directives, or other legal, regulatory, and administrative actions and proceedings cannot be predicted with any degree of certainty. Any claims, lawsuits, investigations, orders, directives, regulatory actions, or proceedings against us, whether meritorious or not, could adversely affect our revenue, cause potential customers to pause sales discussions, cause existing customers to delay, pause or cancel contracts or seek to modify their contracts, undermine our ability to pursue future contracts, damage our relationships with partners and customers, adversely affect our institutional standing, undermine investor confidence, limit our ability to sell our models, platform, and applications in certain industries, result in costly litigation or regulatory or other defense, adversely affect our reputation, require significant management attention, consume significant time, and divert significant resources.

Determining reserves for our pending litigation or other disputes is a complex and fact-intensive process that requires significant subjective judgment and speculation. It is possible that a resolution of one or more such proceedings could result in substantial damages, settlement costs, fines, and penalties that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations. Because of the potential risks, expenses, and uncertainties of litigation or other dispute resolution processes, we may, from time to time, settle disputes, even where we have meritorious claims or defenses, by agreeing to settlement agreements, as we did, for example, in Bartz. Moreover, as in Bartz, insurance may not fully cover such claims, provide sufficient payments to cover all the costs to resolve one or more such claims, or continue to be available on terms acceptable to us. A claim brought against us that is uninsured or underinsured could result in unanticipated costs. These proceedings could also result in harm to our reputation and brand, sanctions, consent decrees, injunctions, or other orders requiring a change in our business practices. Further, under certain circumstances, we have contractual and other legal obligations to indemnify and to incur legal expenses on behalf of our business and commercial partners and current and former directors and officers. Any of these consequences could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

We are subject to anti-corruption and anti-bribery laws, anti-money laundering laws, and similar laws.

We are required to comply with the United States Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of 1977, the United Kingdom Bribery Act 2010, and other anti-corruption and anti-bribery laws, anti-money laundering laws, and similar laws in the United States and non-U.S. countries where we have operations and conduct business. Anti- corruption and anti-bribery laws, which have been enforced aggressively and are interpreted broadly, prohibit companies and their employees, agents, intermediaries, and other third parties from, among other things, promising, authorizing, making, or offering improper payments or other benefits to government officials and other persons in the public and private sector.

We leverage third parties, including intermediaries and agents, to conduct our business and to sell our models, platform, and applications, and in connection with lobbying and other activities. We and these third parties have in the past and may in the future have direct or indirect interactions with officials and employees of government agencies, including state-owned or state-affiliated entities, and we potentially could be held liable for the corrupt or other illegal activities of third-party business partners and intermediaries, our employees, representatives, contractors, partners, agents, and other third parties, even if we do not explicitly authorize such parties’ activities.

We cannot ensure that our policies and procedures to address compliance with these anti-corruption and anti- money laundering laws will be effective, or that all of our employees, representatives, contractors, partners, agents, intermediaries, or other third parties have not taken, or will not take, actions that violate these laws, for which we may be ultimately held liable.

As we increase our non-U.S. sales and business, our risk of violating these laws may increase. Noncompliance with applicable anti-corruption and anti-bribery laws, anti-money laundering, and similar laws could subject us to criminal or civil sanctions, settlements, prosecution, loss of export privileges, suspension or debarment from U.S. government contracts, other enforcement actions, disgorgement of profits, significant fines, damages, other civil and criminal penalties or injunctions, whistleblower complaints, adverse media coverage, and other consequences. Any investigations, actions, or sanctions could adversely affect our reputation, business, financial condition, and results of operations. Responding to any investigation or action could result in a

Governmental trade controls, including export and import controls, sanctions, customs requirements, and related regimes, could subject us to liability or loss of contracting privileges or limit our ability to compete in certain markets.

Our models, platform, and applications are subject to U.S. export controls, and our current or future models, platform, and applications may be controlled under the Export Administration Regulations administered by the U.S. Department of Commerce or, in more limited circumstances, under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (the “ITAR”) administered by the U.S. Department of State. Because the capabilities of our AI models are currently evolving rapidly through ongoing training and frequent model development, and because the export control treatment of AI technologies under relevant laws is developing, the classification of, and controls related to, our models, platform, applications, and technologies under these regimes is inherently uncertain and is likely to change over time. Certain of our models, platform, and applications, and the underlying technology and source code associated with our models, platform, and applications, may require authorization for export, re-export, or transfer in-country, and for individuals of certain nationalities to access such technology or source code, including within the United States.

In particular, our core models, platform, and applications leverage encryption for data protection, and some of our future models, platform, and applications may be able to produce export-controlled software, which may subject exports, re-exports, and transfer of such models, platform, and applications to export authorization requirements.

Our commercially available models, platform, and applications generally can be exported without an export license. However, some exports may require us to obtain authorization from the cognizant government agency. In certain contexts, the provision of our models, platform, and applications may constitute export of defense articles or defense services subject to the ITAR, which can be exported only if we obtain required export licenses or other government authorizations. Compliance with applicable export controls may delay the introduction or sale of our models, platform, and applications outside the United States, restrict our customers’ ability to deploy our models, platform, and applications globally, inhibit our ability to engage in certain research and development activities, limit the performance of our models, platform, and applications, or prohibit exports to certain jurisdictions altogether. Such restrictions have already affected our offerings directly. For example, in June 2026, the U.S. Department of Commerce imposed worldwide export restrictions on our Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models. To ensure compliance, we disabled Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all of our customers; access to our other models was not affected. We re-deployed the Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models after we updated our safeguards in coordination with the U.S. government and our industry partners and once the U.S. Department of Commerce lifted its restrictions. Similar export controls could recur, could remain in place longer, or could extend to additional models or capabilities. In addition, future updates to our safeguards or other remedial measures may not be sufficient to obtain relief from such restrictions or relief may not be granted on a similar timeline or at all. The restrictions imposed in June 2026 and potential restrictions in the future may also limit our ability to recruit talent, to fully utilize our existing workforce, to continue to improve our models, and to make our products available internationally. Any such restrictions could adversely affect our financial results; impair the research, compute, and development investment our models represent; and cause us to lose customers, partners, and market share, including to competitors not subject to similar restrictions.

Further, our activities are subject to economic, financial, and trade sanctions administered by the United States and other countries. Economic, financial, and trade sanctions restrict or prohibit providing certain services and engaging in certain transactions or other activities or dealings with individuals, entities, organizations, governments, territories, and countries targeted by or the subject of these sanctions. These restrictions, as well as other applicable international trade rules, are subject to change with little to no notice. In addition, U.S. law increasingly authorizes the U.S. government to restrict or review the use of hardware, software, and services originating from, or linked to, countries it designates as adversaries, including such technology incorporated into the supply chains for the data centers and compute infrastructure on which we depend. Such restrictions could narrow our choice of suppliers, delay our infrastructure projects, or increase our costs, and any noncompliance could result in penalties, loss of contracting privileges, or reputational harm.

We take various steps to comply with applicable export controls and sanctions, including: maintaining a global trade policy and a supported regions policy, implementing IP-based geo-blocking controls that restrict access from certain jurisdictions to our models, platform, and applications, conducting restricted party screening of certain customers and vendors, and including sanctions and export compliance provisions in our customer terms of service and vendor agreements. However, we cannot guarantee the compliance measures and internal controls we have adopted will prevent violations of applicable export controls and sanctions. Our compliance measures and internal controls have not always been and may not always be fully effective, and we have in the past voluntarily disclosed, and may in the future voluntarily disclose, instances of noncompliance to the relevant authorities. We cannot guarantee that our measures will prevent future violations or keep pace with the changing substance, application, and interpretation of export controls and sanctions. In addition, third parties have in the past and may in the future take action to circumvent our compliance measures and controls in order to gain access to our products and services.

If we misclassify a product or service, export, re-export, transfer, or provide access to a product, or provide services in violation of applicable export controls or sanctions, or otherwise fail to comply with applicable export controls or sanctions, we could be subject to criminal or civil sanctions, settlements, prosecution, loss of export privileges, suspension or debarment from U.S. government contracts, other enforcement actions, disgorgement of profits, significant fines, damages, other civil and criminal penalties or injunctions, whistleblower complaints, adverse media coverage, and other consequences. Any investigations, actions, or sanctions could adversely affect our reputation, business, financial condition, and results of operations. Responding to any investigation or action could result in a significant diversion of management’s attention and resources and significant defense costs and other professional fees.

Decreased use of our models, platform, and applications by customers outside the United States or a limitation on our ability to export, provide access to, or sell our models, platform, and applications to customers and other parties outside the United States may cause our revenue from such unsupported jurisdictions to decline and otherwise adversely affect our business and financial condition.

If we or our business partners or counterparties, including licensors and licensees, prime contractors, subcontractors, sublicensors, vendors, customers, or contractors, violate export controls or sanctions, including by failing to obtain appropriate import, export, or re-export licenses or permits, or if we fail to secure such contractual commitments from such parties, we may also be adversely affected through reputational harm as well as other negative consequences, including government investigations or penalties.

Negative consequences stemming from violations or apparent violations of export controls or sanctions may include the loss of the right to sell our models, platform, and applications or services to the U.S. government, or to other U.S. or non-U.S. public bodies, or a reduction in our ability to compete for such sales opportunities involving government agencies or other public bodies. Further, ensuring that we are complying with applicable export controls and sanctions regulations may be time-consuming and could result in the delay or loss of sales opportunities in connection with particular transactions.

Also, in addition to the United States, various countries regulate the import and export of certain encryption and other technology and have enacted laws that could limit our ability to distribute our models, platform, and applications or could limit our customers’ abilities to implement our models, platform, and applications in those countries. For example, following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, the United States and other countries imposed financial and trade sanctions and export controls against Russia, Belarus, and certain regions of Ukraine, and the United States and other countries could impose wider sanctions and export restrictions and take other actions should the conflict further escalate. Any new export restrictions, new legislation, changes in sanctions, or shifting approaches in the enforcement or scope of existing regulations, or in the individuals, entities, governments, territories, countries, or technologies targeted by or subject to such regulations could result in decreased use of our models, platform, and applications by existing customers outside of the United States, reduced adoption of our models, platform, and applications by new customers with non-U.S. operations, limited expansion into new markets, and decreased revenue.

To continue to grow our business, we will need to continue to expand internationally, and our non-U.S. operations and sales to customers outside of the United States expose us to risks inherent in international expansion, sales, and operations.

We are a U.S.-headquartered company with extensive U.S. operations, as well as subsidiaries and operations in several other countries and regions, including the UK, the EU, Canada, and Asia-Pacific, and we expect that our future growth will depend in part on expanding our operations, customer base, and market presence in additional jurisdictions. Operating in non-U.S. markets requires significant resources and management attention and subjects us to regulatory, economic, and political risks that are different from those in the United States. As we continue to expand internationally, we have faced and will continue to face risks in doing business internationally that could adversely affect our business, including:

•the need to localize and adapt our models, platform, and applications for specific countries, including

translation into foreign languages and associated expenses;

•privacy laws, export controls, sanctions, and other national security regulations that impose different

and potentially conflicting obligations with respect to how personal data is processed or require that customer data be stored in a designated territory;

•laws in certain jurisdictions that may compel our personnel, vendors, or facility operators to provide

governmental authorities with access to our systems, data, or other information, in some cases without notice to us and with limited means of recourse;

•national security-driven data security regulations. For example, the Department of Justice issued a

final rule, effective in April 2025, that places limitations, and in some cases prohibitions, on certain transfers of covered data to partners, service providers, and vendors, employees, and investors located in China or with other specified links to China (and other designated countries). We may be required to re-examine current contracts or engage in new contract negotiations with third parties that aid in processing data on our behalf or otherwise have access to covered data. These rules also may broadly require us to extract promises from other third-party service providers that they will not transfer data we share with them onward to parties linked to countries of concern;

•import regulations, including U.S. and non-U.S. governments’ restrictive trade policies, customs duties

and other parallel regulations, tariffs and counter-measures;

•AI, data-related, cybersecurity and digital service laws and regulations (such as the EU AI Act, NIS2,

the EU Digital Services Act, the UK Online Safety Act 2023, and the EU Data Act) that are rapidly evolving, require continuous monitoring to ensure constant compliance maturity level, and often provide for substantial penalties in the event of a violation;

•difficulties in staffing and managing non-U.S. operations, including the complexity of engaging in

various employer-of-record arrangements that are geographically dispersed, and the risk that these arrangements, or any failure to establish local employing entities on a timely basis, may not comply with local employment, labor, or tax laws, exposing us to fines, penalties, reclassification or back- payment claims;

•difficulties of maintaining our culture across various jurisdictions;

•regulatory and other delays and difficulties in setting up non-U.S. operations, including delays in

forming, registering, and obtaining governmental approvals for local subsidiaries, delays in establishing local banking, payroll, and benefits arrangements, and difficulties relating to safety of personnel and information, any of which could delay local hiring efforts;

•the potential need for reviews by the U.S. government or non-U.S. governments of certain transactions

with non-U.S. entities;

•different pricing environments, longer sales cycles, longer accounts receivable payment cycles, and

collections issues;

Entry into certain transactions with non-U.S. entities or persons may be subject to government regulations and review, including review by U.S. or non-U.S. government entities related to contemplated or past foreign direct investment (“FDI”). In recent years, many countries around the world have adopted FDI regulatory regimes that seek to scrutinize investments into, and acquisitions of, domestic companies by non-domestic parties, granting governments broad discretion to review contemplated and past investment and acquisition transactions. These FDI regimes may restrict our ability to attract investments, invest in or fund partners outside of the United States that build or operate data centers or supply infrastructure for us, or acquire promising new technologies, companies, and talent in countries around the world, potentially limiting our ability to carry out our business strategy.

More broadly, increasing geopolitical tensions, shifting national security priorities, and evolving technology- focused trade, export control, investment, and data-governance regimes may result in restrictions, heightened scrutiny, or other limitations on our ability to operate, sell products, partner with customers, or deploy our models in certain jurisdictions or with certain counterparties. Governments may adopt or expand policies aimed at protecting domestic technology ecosystems, restricting the cross-border flow of data, or limiting access to AI systems, which could reduce our addressable market, increase compliance costs, or require operational changes. In particular, the U.S. government has increasingly scrutinized AI companies’ exposure to Chinese state actors, and future executive orders, legislation, or regulatory actions could restrict our ability to serve customers or users connected to China, impose new compliance obligations, lead us to implement anti-abuse mechanisms or other technical access restrictions, or limit the deployment of our models in ways that reduce our addressable market. Any escalation of U.S.-China tensions, retaliatory measures by either government, or failure or perceived failure by us to prevent non-U.S. state-actor access to or misuse of our technology could subject us to regulatory action, reputational harm, and adverse effects on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

As we continue to expand our business globally, our success will depend, in large part, on our ability to anticipate and effectively manage these risks. These factors and others could adversely affect our ability to increase non-U.S. revenue and, consequently, could materially impact our business. Increasing scrutiny and changing expectations from global regulations, our investors, customers, and employees with respect to ESG may result in additional compliance risk and costs and may impact our reputation and business.

Companies across many industries are facing increasing scrutiny related to their environmental, social, and governance (“ESG”) practices and reporting, both in the United States and internationally. For example, new U.S. and non-U.S. laws and regulations relating to ESG matters, including environmental sustainability and climate change, are under consideration, being adopted, or being rolled back or revised, which may impact our necessary disclosures and increase compliance costs. Further, there is particular focus on concerns relating to AI and its impact on society and the environment, including the power-intensive nature of the industry, public concern regarding high consumption of water, and reliance on critical minerals and rare elements in the manufacture of chips. Some investors may use these non-financial performance factors to guide their investment strategies and, in some cases, may choose not to invest in us if they believe our policies and actions relating to ESG are inadequate.

Our sustainability initiatives, goals, or commitments could be difficult to achieve or costly to implement. Moreover, compliance with recently adopted and potential upcoming ESG requirements, including California legislation that requires various climate-related disclosures, the EU’s Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive and Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive, and the UK’s Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting framework will require the dedication of significant time and resources. At the same time, anti-ESG sentiment has gained momentum across the United States, particularly at the federal level, and the federal and certain state governments have enacted or proposed “anti-ESG” policies or legislation or have issued related legal opinions. Such policies, legislation, initiatives, and scrutiny could expose any sustainability, employment, governance, or other corporate practices we adopt to the risk of litigation, antitrust investigations or challenges by federal or state authorities, and result in injunctions, penalties, and reputational harm.

Additionally, if our competitors’ corporate social responsibility performance is perceived to be better than ours, potential or current investors may elect to invest with our competitors instead. Our business may face increased scrutiny related to these activities and our related disclosures, including from the investment community and the local communities in which we operate, and our failure to achieve progress or manage the dynamic, and at times conflicting, public sentiment and legal landscape in these areas on a timely basis, or at all, could adversely affect our reputation, business, and financial condition. Further, we may experience backlash from customers, government entities, advocacy groups, employees, or other stakeholders who disagree with our actual or perceived positions, or with our lack of position on social, environmental, governance, political, public policy, economic, geopolitical, or other sensitive issues. Any perceived lack of transparency about these matters could adversely affect our brand and reputation, our employees’ engagement and retention, and the willingness of our customers and partners to do business with us. Both advocates and opponents to ESG-related matters are increasingly resorting to a range of activism forms, including media campaigns and litigation, to advance their perspectives. To the extent we are subject to such activism, it may require us to incur costs or otherwise adversely affect our business, financial condition, or results of operations.

If we or our payment processing partners fail to manage the risks related to our payment processing activities, we could be subject to fines, regulatory scrutiny, disruptions to our business, or reputational harm.

A portion of our customers authorize us to bill their credit card accounts through our third-party payment processing partners for our paid subscriptions. We have experienced, and expect to continue to experience, fraudulent activity, including the creation of accounts using false identities and the use of stolen or otherwise unauthorized payment credentials, which has resulted, and may in the future result, in losses, including payment processing fees, chargeback fees, and fines and penalties imposed by our payment processors or card networks, for which we may not be reimbursed. We maintain fraud-detection and identity-verification measures, but these measures have not identified, and may not identify, all such activity. Further, our customers provide us with credit card billing information online, and we do not verify the physical identity of cardholders or conduct additional verification beyond what we collect online, which increases our risk of exposure to fraudulent activity.

We also incur chargebacks when customers or card issuers claim that a transaction was unauthorized or otherwise improper. If the volume of chargebacks were to become excessive, we could be subject to higher processing fees, additional fines, fraud mitigation requirements, or the termination or suspension of our ability to accept certain payment methods, including credit cards, through one or more payment processors.

In addition, payment processors, card networks, and financial institutions periodically revise their rules, standards, and practices, including data protection and documentation standards, required to utilize their services from time to time. Our compliance with these evolving requirements, and the continued compliance of our third-party payment processing partners, may require additional operational resources and expense. Failure by us or our payment processing partners to comply with applicable standards could result in fines, increased scrutiny, disruptions to our business and our ability to process payments for our intelligence service, or reputational harm.
